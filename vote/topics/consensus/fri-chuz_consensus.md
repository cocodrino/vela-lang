---

## Unanimous Agreements (all 5)

1. `fri-chuz` is **phonotactically and semantically valid** as a compound for "free will."
2. **Both roots exist** in LEXICON_BASE.md (`fri` = free, `chuz` = choose) — no new atoms needed.
3. An **atomic loan** or `freewil` is **fatally flawed** — structural homonymy with future suffix `-wil`.
4. `fri-chuz` and `fri.dom` (freedom) are **semantically distinct** — state vs. faculty.
5. `fri chuz` as separate words is **insufficient** — produces a verb phrase, not the nominal philosophical concept.

---

## The Conflict: Orthography

**Morphologist's formal position:** GRAMMAR_COMPLETE.md §15.1 mandates root+root compounds fuse without hyphen (`frichuz`). `fri` is an adjective root, not a prefix, so the no-hyphen rule applies.

**Countervailing evidence:**
- The entire existing lexicon (LEXICON_BASE.md + LEXICON_EXTENDED.md) uses hyphens for root+root compounds: `kom-hom`, `lern-hous`, `test-kod`, `brain-chip`, `piktur-chip`, `wok-mem`, `data-disk`, `port-konekt`, `skrin-toch`, `lowd-in`, `lowd-up`, `klaud-sav`, `IP-adres`, `apli-brij`, `servis-mikro`, `lod-balans`, `masin-lern`, `brain-net`, `lern-modl`, `lern-data`, `ai-instruk`, `auto-lern`, `fol-fors`, `lif-lern`, `art-hous`, `muzik-mak-po`, `vote-po`, `tru-nes`, `nof-nes`, `dep-nof`...
- Even §15.1's own example `wotc-man` contradicts its rule text.
- The Aestheticist and Lexicographer both prefer hyphenated form for transparency.

**Morphologist's pragmatic recommendation:** "Option B (universal hyphenation) is least disruptive and maximizes morphological transparency."

---

## Resolution

**Default to pragmatic consistency:** Approve `fri-chuz` (hyphenated), overriding the formal §15.1 rule for now, because:
- Changing the entire lexicon's orthography is out of scope for a single-word deliberation
- Universal hyphenation is already the de facto standard
- The Morphologist himself recommends this as the least disruptive path

**Follow-up action:** Schedule a SYSTEMIC ORTHOGRAPHY DELIBERATION (Phase 5b or 6) to resolve §15.1 vs. actual lexicon inconsistency. Until then, all new compounds follow existing hyphen convention.
