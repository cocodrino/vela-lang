# R21 — Phonologist ruling
**Date:** 2026-10-26 · Claude subagent, read-only · **FINAL VOTE: Q1=B (-edo), Q2=B**

**Dossier corrections (verified by the specialist):** PHONOLOGY_FINAL contradicts itself: §3.2 allows {n,m,l,r,s}; §11 (l.467) and §12 (l.494) list only n/m/l/r (no /s/); §3.1 (l.127) lists `pant, send` as valid. The committee missed `-ed` first: R8 (`fase5_consensus.md:161`) calls `hav-ed` /ha.ved/ "legal", but /d/ is word-final. `TOLERATED_CODA` in the validator also includes `ng`.

**Is final /d/ hard?** Spanish weakens or drops it (`hav-ed` → [ha.βe]); Japanese inserts a vowel ([ha.be.do]); Mandarin drops or devoices it. Hindi and Arabic are fine. So 3 of 5 target groups alter the only cue for past tense, in the weakest perceptual position.

**Q1 = B, `-edo` /e.do/.** All syllables open; penultimate stress lands on the suffix vowel: `mak-edo` /ma.ˈke.do/, `hav-edo`, `si-edo`, `es-edo`. It is what Japanese learners already produce and echoes Spanish -ado/-ido. Rejects `-eda` (looks like the R7 citation vowel; clashes with -ed participle headwords `tireda`, `boreda`, `afreda`, `deda`), `-de` (= pronoun `de`), `-do` (compound element in `bak-do`, `agen-do`), `-di`/`-edi` (-i looks like an adjective ending: `redi`, `hapi`; Japanese /di/ → [dʑi]). Risk: `-edo` vs locative `-to` for Mandarin listeners (the extra /e/ syllable separates them). Amendment: fix §3.1 `pant/send`, add /s/ to §11/§12, errata note for R8 l.161.

**Q2 = B.** `es` legal and already regular; zero irregular verbs. Reverse the ❌ on `es-wil`, remove `wil es`, rewrite FAQ l.1072.
