# Phonologist Review: fri-chuz

## Verdict
APPROVE

## Confidence
High

## Phonotactic Analysis
- `/fri.chuz/` syllabifies as **CCV.CVC**
- Onset cluster `/fr/` already established in inventory (`fri` root = `/frai/`)
- Coda `/z/` already established (`chuz` root = `/chuz/`)
- Syllable boundary falls between vowel `/i/` and consonant `/ch/` (**V.C**), which is maximally unambiguous
- No cross-syllable consonant clusters; zero resyllabification risk
- Both vowels `/i/` and `/u/` are within documented VELA inventory
- Orthographic hyphenation does not alter phonological wordhood

## Articulatory Assessment
- Transition `/i/` (close front unrounded) → `/ch/` (postalveolar affricate): tongue body stays **high** throughout; only front-to-postalveolar retraction required
- Comparable sequence exists in English **"each"** `/iːtʃ/` — cross-linguistically natural and common
- No laryngeal switching cost (`/f/` voiceless → `/ch/` voiceless → `/z/` voiced is the only switch, occurring across a syllable boundary where it is effortless)
- `/fr/` onset: labiodental + postalveolar approximant; present in major world languages (English *free*, Spanish *frente*, French *frère*, German *frei*, Mandarin 夫人 *fūren*)
- `/z/` coda: voiced alveolar fricative; universally accessible, though some L1 Mandarin speakers may devoice it slightly — still perceptually recoverable
- Overall articulatory cost: **low**

## Perceptual Salience
- **Syllable boundary**: V.C junction provides 100% acoustic clarity; no ambisyllabic consonant to obscure the break
- **Vowel contrast**: `/i/` (front, unrounded) vs. `/u/` (back, rounded) are **maximally distinct** on two dimensions (backness + rounding), giving high perceptual distance even though both are high vowels
- **Affricate cue**: `/ch/` provides a strong acoustic burst + frication, marking syllable onset unambiguously
- **No homophonic clash**: distinct from existing `fri.dom` (`/fri.dom/`) in the entire second syllable

## Aesthetic Quality
- **Vowel arc**: `/i/` → `/u/` creates a smooth, melodic front-to-back glide within the high-vowel space; avoids the "compressed" feel because the backness/rounding difference is phonetically substantial
- **Rhythm**: With VELA pitch accent on the second syllable, the word gains an **iambic** contour (`da-DUM`), lending philosophical weight to the second element (`chuz` = choice)
- **Texture**: `/f/` (soft labiodental) → `/ch/` (crisp postalveolar affricate) → `/z/` (voiced continuous fricative) gives a **soft–crisp–flowing** trajectory
- **Length**: 2 syllables, 2 roots — balanced; neither telegraphic nor ponderous
- **Typological parallel**: Comparable in melodic contour to Japanese *jiyū* (freedom) or Swahili *hi-fu* structures, both judged euphonic

## Problems Identified
None significant. Minor learner notes only:

1. **Stress accommodation**: Speakers with fixed initial-stress L1 habits may briefly place prominence on `/fri/` rather than `/chuz/`. This is a general VELA learning curve issue for all iambic compounds, not specific to this word.
2. **High-vowel register**: Both syllables occupy the high-vowel acoustic space; this limits sonority expansion compared to a low-vowel compound. However, this is an inherent property of the semantically optimal roots `fri` + `chuz`, not a phonotactic defect.

`/fri.chuz/` is **phonotactically valid, articulatorily effortless, perceptually salient, and aesthetically balanced**. The V.C syllable boundary is maximally clear, the vowel transition `/i/`→`/u/` is melodically smooth, and both roots are fully established in the VELA inventory. No phonological barriers to adoption. Approved for lexicalization as the compound for *free will / libre albedrío*.
