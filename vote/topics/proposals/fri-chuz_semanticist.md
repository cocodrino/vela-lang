## Compositionality Assessment

The compound `fri-chuz` decomposes into:

- **fri** /frai/ — adjective "free, unconstrained," type ⟨e, t⟩ modifier
- **chuz** /chuz/ — verb "to choose, to select among alternatives," type ⟨e, ⟨e, t⟩⟩

**Literal compositional meaning:** `fri`(choose) → "choose freely" → nominalized: "free choice" (the act or capacity of choosing without external constraint).

**Target meaning:** "free will" (the metaphysical faculty or capacity to make choices not determined by prior causes; or, in compatibilist readings, the capacity to act according to one's own motivations).

### The Metonymic Gap: Small and Cross-Linguistically Motivated

The extension from "free choice" → "free will" involves a single, minimal shift:

> **Instance/Capacity → Faculty**: from "the act/capacity of choosing freely" to "the underlying faculty that enables free choice."

This is a standard **faculty-for-act** metonymy (cf. "she has good **sight**" = "she has the faculty of seeing well"). It is the same metonymy that operates in many natural language compounds for this concept:

| Language | Term | Literal Gloss | Metonymic Step |
|----------|------|---------------|----------------|
| Latin | *liberum arbitrium* | "free judgment / free choice" | choice → faculty of choice |
| Spanish | *libre albedrío* | "free choice / free arbitration" | choice → faculty of choice |
| French | *libre arbitre* | "free arbitration / free choice" | choice → faculty of choice |
| VELA | **fri-chuz** | "free choose" → "free choice" | choice → faculty of choice |

**Contrast with `self-nof`:**
`self-nof` required **two** metonymic shifts (act→capacity, specific→general) to reach "consciousness." `fri-chuz` requires only **one** well-attested shift (choice→faculty-of-choice). The gap is substantially smaller and recoverable from the morphemes alone: a language learner who knows `fri` and `chuz` can deduce "capacity to choose freely" with minimal effort.

**Verdict on compositionality:** ✅ **PASSES.** The composition is transparent, the metonymic step is minimal and cross-linguistically standard.

## Polysemy Risk Analysis

### Risk: LOW ✅

`fri-chuz` has essentially **one** reading with two natural nuances:

| Reading | Gloss | Semantic Type | Naturalness |
|---------|-------|---------------|-------------|
| **R1: Free choice (instance)** | "a choice made without constraint" | eventive instance | `Mi mak un fri-chuz.` → "I made a free choice." |
| **R2: Free will (faculty)** | "the capacity to make free choices" | faculty/abstract | `La fri-chuz es wat mak wi moral.` → "Free will is what makes us moral." |

These are NOT distinct lexical meanings. They are the **instance/faculty polysemy** found in hundreds of English nouns: *decision* (an instance of deciding vs. the faculty of decision-making), *choice* (a particular selection vs. the power to choose), *judgment* (a verdict vs. the capacity to judge).

In English, "free choice" and "free will" are separate lexical items. But this separation is a historical accident — "will" is a Germanic word, "choice" is from French — not a semantic necessity. VELA's transparent compound recovers the semantic unity that English obscures.

### Risk with `fri.dom`

| Concept | VELA | Orthography | Semantic Domain |
|---------|------|-------------|-----------------|
| Freedom | `fri.dom` | `.` (syllable boundary in loan) | **state** — being unconstrained |
| Free will | `fri-chuz` | `-` (compound marker) | **faculty** — capacity to choose freely |

**Orthographic distinction:** The `.` vs. `-` distinction provides immediate visual disambiguation. A reader cannot confuse `fri.dom` (syllable boundary within a loan-derived word) with `fri-chuz` (explicit compound marker).

**Semantic distinction:**
- You can have `fri.dom` (freedom from prison, external constraints) and still lack `fri-chuz` in some philosophical frameworks (e.g., hard determinism holds that no one has free will even if they are "free" from external coercion).
- Conversely, you can lack `fri.dom` (imprisoned) but still possess `fri-chuz` (you can choose your attitude, your beliefs, etc.).
- The state/faculty distinction is robust and cross-linguistically stable.

**Risk verdict:** LOW. No genuine polysemy, no collision with `fri.dom`.

### Internal Type Pattern

Does `fri-chuz` follow the internal hyponym pattern of VELA compounds?
- `lern-hous` ⊆ `hous` (a school IS a house for learning)
- `fol-fors` ⊆ `fors` (gravity IS a force)
- `fri-chuz` ⊆ `chuz`? — is free will a type of choosing?

This is the trickiest test. "Free will" is not exactly "a type of choosing." It is "the capacity that **enables** choosing." This is a **faculty-enablement** relation, not strict hyponymy.

However, the same pattern appears in `self-nof` (consciousness is not "a type of knowing") and in many compounds cross-linguistically. Abstract compounds often express **faculty-of-Root** or **capacity-for-Root** rather than strict hyponymy. This is a systematic extension of the compound pattern into abstract domains, not an isolated violation.

**Verdict:** Type-consistent at the syntactic and semantic levels. The internal hyponymy pattern weakens for abstract compounds — this is expected and systematic, not ad-hoc.

| Feature | `fri.dom` (freedom) | `fri-chuz` (free will) |

## The `wil` Structural Homonymy Analysis

### Current State

`wil` /wil/ is the future tense suffix: `Mi go-wil` = "I will go."

### Hypothetical Risk

A naive compound `fri-wil` (free + future) would be problematic — it would be structurally ambiguous between "free future" and (for an English speaker) "free will."

### Why `fri-chuz` Avoids This

The proposal uses `chuz` (choose), not `wil` (future). The compound `fri-chuz` has **zero** structural homonymy with the tense system:

- `Mi fri-chuz` → "I have free will" (noun predicate) — unambiguous
- `Mi fri-chuz-wil` → "I will have free will" (future tense of noun predicate) — unambiguous
- `Mi fri chuz-wil la fru.` → "I will freely choose the fruit." (adverb + future verb) — not `fri-chuz`, separate words

No collision, no ambiguity. ✅

### Note for Future

If VELA ever needs "free will" as a theological term (e.g., "the will that is free"), consider `fri-vol` (free + volition). But `fri-chuz` handles the philosophical concept without touching the `wil` suffix. Good design.

## `chuz` vs. `disaid` — Root Choice Validation

VELA has two relevant verbs in this semantic field:

- **chuz** /chuz/ — "to choose, select among alternatives" (focus on selection from a set)
- **disaid** /disaid/ — "to decide, reach a conclusion" (focus on cognitive resolution)

**Why `chuz` is correct for "free will":**

Free will is fundamentally about **choice** (selection among genuine alternatives), not mere decision (reaching a cognitive conclusion). The philosophical problem of free will centers on whether we have *alternative possibilities* — whether we *could have chosen otherwise*. `chuz` (selection from alternatives) captures this better than `disaid` (resolution).

Cross-linguistic evidence:
- Latin: *liberum **arbitrium*** — from *arbiter* = "judge, chooser" (selection among options)
- Spanish: *libre **albedrío*** — from Latin *arbitrium* = "choice, judgment"
- Greek: *αὐτεξούσιον* (autexousion) — "self-authorized choice"

`disaid` would yield `fri-disaid` = "free decision" — a valid compound for a different concept (the ability to decide without coercion), but not "free will" in its philosophical sense.

**Verdict:** `chuz` is the correct root choice. ✅

## Problems Identified

### Problem 1 (MINOR): Instance/Faculty Ambiguity

`fri-chuz` can mean both "a free choice" (instance) and "free will" (faculty). As noted above, this is standard instance/faculty polysemy and not a lexical ambiguity. However, in some contexts a disambiguating compound form might help.

**Severity:** LOW — normal linguistic behavior, shared with `choice`, `decision`, `judgment` in English.

**Mitigation:** Context disambiguates. If needed, `fri-chuz-instans` (free choice instance) or explicit quantifiers (`un fri-chuz` = one free choice) can clarify.

### Problem 2 (MINOR): `fri-chuz` and `fri.dom` Adjacency in Lexicon

Both start with `fri`, both are abstract nouns in the philosophical domain. A learner might briefly confuse them.

**Severity:** LOW — resolved by orthographic distinction (`.` vs. `-`) and clear semantic boundary (state vs. faculty).

### Problem 3 (COSMETIC): Abstract Compound Type Pattern

`fri-chuz` follows the Adj+V → N pattern, which is attested but not heavily populated in the lexicon. The internal hyponymy pattern (compound ⊆ head) weakens for abstract compounds. This is systematic (not ad-hoc) but worth noting for compound theory documentation.

**Severity:** NEGLIGIBLE — expected behavior for abstract compounds cross-linguistically.

## Rationale Summary

`fri-chuz` is a **well-formed, compositionally transparent, semantically motivated** VELA compound.

**Strengths:**

1. **Compositionality passes:** The metonymic gap from "free choice" to "free will" is a single, minimal, cross-linguistically standard step (choice → faculty-of-choice). Far smaller than the gap required for `self-nof` → "consciousness."

2. **Monosemy preserved:** The instance/faculty ambiguity is standard productive polysemy, not lexical ambiguity. No new morpheme meanings are required.

3. **Type consistency holds:** Adj+V → N is a valid VELA compound pattern, yielding an abstract noun of type ⟨e⟩.

4. **No polysemy with `fri.dom`:** Orthographic distinction (`.` vs. `-`) and clear semantic boundary (state vs. faculty) prevent confusion. Test sentences confirm they are distinct.

5. **`wil` homonymy avoided:** Using `chuz` (choose) instead of `wil` (future suffix) avoids all structural homonymy with the tense system.

6. **Correct root choice:** `chuz` (choose from alternatives) is semantically superior to `disaid` (reach a conclusion) for encoding "free will," matching Latin *arbitrium* pattern.

7. **Natural example exists:** `La diskushon of la fri-chuz es old.` — "The discussion of free will is old." This is a natural, idiomatic sentence.

**Verdict:** APPROVE without modification. `fri-chuz` is one of the strongest compounds in Phase 5 — compositionally transparent, semantically precise, and well-motivated cross-linguistically. It should serve as a **model** for how VELA compounds encode abstract philosophical concepts, in contrast with the more problematic `self-nof`.
