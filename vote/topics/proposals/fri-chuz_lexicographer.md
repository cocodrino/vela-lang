# Lexicographer Review: fri-chuz

## Verdict
APPROVE — with flagged AFI collision for follow-up

## Confidence
High

## Pan-Recognizability Assessment

### `fri` (adjective "free") — Score: 8/10
Excellent. The root is among the most universally recognizable morphemes in VELA.
- **English:** "free" /friː/ — immediate. The VELA spelling `fri` retains the critical consonant frame /f-r-i/.
- **Germanic family:** German *frei*, Dutch *vrij*, Swedish *fri*, Danish *fri*, Norwegian *fri*, Icelandic *frjáls*. The form is stable across the entire branch. The /f-r-/ onset cluster is the universal marker.
- **Romance:** No native cognate (Romance uses *libre*/*libero*/*livre*), but "free" is among the top-50 English words globally. L2 English speakers from any language group will recognize it. Spanish speakers specifically encounter "free" in computing (freeware, free software) and commerce (free shipping / *envío gratis*).
- **Sinitic:** Only via English borrowing, but that exposure is massive. Chinese 自由 (zìyóu) has no phonological link, but the VELA form `fri` is learnable as a short high-frequency item.

**Note:** The AFI assignment /frai/ creates a homophone with `frai` ("to fly"). This does not affect `fri`'s recognizability as a *written* morpheme but must be flagged (see Problems Identified below).

### `chuz` (verb "to choose") — Score: 6/10
Moderate-High. Recognizable primarily through English.
- **English:** "choose" /tʃuːz/ — the VELA form `chuz` /chuz/ is phonologically close enough (same /tʃ/ onset, same /z/ coda, vowel shift /uː/ → /u/ is minor). The visual-orthographic link (ch-z) reinforces recognizability.
- **Germanic family:** No close phonological cognate. German *wählen*, Dutch *kiezen*, Swedish *välja* — all different roots. Old English *cēosan* is the historical source of "choose," but the modern Germanic cognates have diverged.
- **Romance:** No phonological link (Spanish *elegir*, French *choisir*, Italian *scegliere* — all from Latin *eligere*). However, the English word "choose" is high-frequency enough that Romance L2 speakers will recognize it with minimal effort.
- **Sinitic/Slavic:** Requires explicit learning. Chinese 选择 (xuǎnzé), Russian выбирать (vybirat'). No native phonological hook.

### Compound `fri-chuz` as a unit — Score: 7/10
- For English speakers: fully transparent ("free" + "choose" → "free choice" → "free will"). The semantic extension from "free choice" to the philosophical concept "free will" is intuitive and requires no leap.
- For Germanic speakers: `fri` is a native cognate; `chuz` requires English exposure but is learnable.
- For Romance speakers: `fri` is English-recognizable; `chuz` requires English exposure. However, the *conceptual* mapping is clear: "libre" + "eligir/choisir" ≈ "libre albedrío/libre arbitre." The compound logic (free + choose → the capacity to choose freely) is universal.
- Cross-linguistic validation: The etymological logic of `fri-chuz` actually aligns with the *semantic core* of the concept better than English "free will" does. English "will" derives from Germanic *willjan* = "to desire/want," not "to choose." Spanish *albedrío* and Italian *arbitrio* both encode *judgment/decision* (closer to "choose" than "will"). `fri-chuz` captures this "free decision" sense more precisely than `freewil` would.

## Compound vs Separate Words

### Option A: `fri-chuz` (hyphenated compound, noun "free will")
- **Pros:**
  - Lexifies a unitary philosophical concept that is treated as a single term across philosophical traditions
  - Parallel to existing `fri.dom` (freedom) — both are `fri-` compounds for abstract concepts
  - Parallel to existing `self-nof` (consciousness) — another philosophical compound in the same Section 5.4
  - Prevents morphosyntactic ambiguity: `fri chuz` (two words) could be read as an adjective + verb phrase ("to freely choose") rather than a noun ("free will")
  - Enables syntactic operations: "La fri-chuz of man es importent" (The free will of man is important) vs. "Man fri chuz" (Man freely chooses) — different constructions, different meanings
- **Cons:**
  - Adds one more hyphenated compound to memorize
  - Could theoretically be expressed periphrastically

### Option B: `fri chuz` (two words, periphrastic)
- **Pros:**
  - Zero lexical overhead — uses existing words in syntax
  - Maximally compositional — meaning is literal ("free choose")
- **Cons:**
  - **Critical syntactic ambiguity:** In VELA, `fri chuz` reads as adjective + verb = "freely choose" (a verb phrase), not a noun. To express "free will" you'd need something like `la fri chuz-nes` but `-nes` is not a productive suffix for verbs.
  - **Philosophical usage requires a nominal:** Philosophers discuss "free will" as a noun (a thing that exists, is debated, can be affirmed or denied). You cannot discuss the *existence* of "freely choosing" as a nominal concept without a compound. Compare:
    - "Does free will exist?" → `Fri-chuz eksist?` (grammatical noun as subject)
    - "Does one freely choose?" → `Man fri chuz?` (different question — about an action, not the philosophical concept)
  - **Precedent:** VELA already compounds abstract philosophical nouns: `self-nof`, `tru-nes`, `nof-nes`, `dep-nof`. Periphrasis was rejected for those; consistency demands the same treatment here.

### Verdict: Compound is necessary.
The philosophical concept "free will" is a nominal — a *thing* under debate — not a verbal process. A hyphenated compound is the correct VELA morphological strategy for lexifying it. The two-word option produces a verb phrase with different semantics.

"Free will" is at least as philosophically central as "consciousness" and "truth." Omitting a dedicated term would create a conspicuous gap in VELA's philosophical vocabulary.

### Semantic relation to existing terms
- `fri.dom` = freedom (the state of being free) — broad, political/social
- `fri-chuz` = free will (the philosophical capacity for uncoerced choice) — narrow, metaphysical

These are **not** synonyms. `fri.dom` = the condition; `fri-chuz` = the faculty. The distinction maps onto:
- English: "freedom" ≠ "free will"
- Spanish: "libertad" ≠ "libre albedrío"
- German: "Freiheit" ≠ "freier Wille"

Both terms are needed. No redundancy.

## False Friends / Cognate Traps

### `fri-` in compounds
- **English "free-":** Productive prefix (free speech, free trade, free fall, free kick). `fri-chuz` will be interpreted correctly by English speakers.
- **No conflicting cognates found:** No known language where a form similar to /frai/ or /fri/ combined with a form similar to /chuz/ produces a conflicting meaning.

### `chuz` standalone
- **English "choose":** Direct — no trap.
- **German:** No phonologically similar common word. *Klaus* /kaʊs/ is distant.
- **Dutch:** None.
- **Turkish:** *Seç* /setʃ/ — different.
- **Arabic:** None.
- **Japanese:** *Chūzu* does not exist natively; English borrowing "choose" would be recognized.

### Compound `fri-chuz` as a unit
- **No phonological overlap with any natural language compound found.**
- Closest: English "free choice" — semantically identical, not a false friend.
- No risk of the compound being misinterpreted as something else in any surveyed language.

### AFI homophone issue (flagged, not a false friend)
- `fri` = /frai/ and `frai` = /frai/ (verb "to fly") — **homophones in speech**
- In the compound `fri-chuz`, context eliminates the ambiguity: `frai-chuz` ("fly-choose") is semantically incoherent; `fri-chuz` ("free-choose") is coherent. No real-world confusion likely.
- However, this homophone is a **systemic issue** (two distinct morphemes sharing one AFI). It should be resolved at the atom level in a future AFI audit, not at the compound level.
- **Impact on this review:** Minimal. The written forms `fri` and `frai` are distinct; the compound's meaning is clear in both written and spoken forms.

## Problems Identified

### 1. AFI collision: `fri` /frai/ = `frai` /frai/
**Severity: Low (compound-level), Medium (system-level)**
Two atoms share the same AFI. This is an imperfection in VELA's phonological system. In the specific case of `fri-chuz`, the collision does not cause ambiguity because:
- `frai` ("to fly") + `chuz` ("to choose") = a semantically incoherent compound
- `fri` ("free") + `chuz` ("to choose") = a semantically coherent compound
- Grammatical position also disambiguates: `fri` is an adjective (modifying `chuz`), while `frai` is a verb

**Recommendation:** Flag for Phase 6 AFI audit. The `fri` AFI might be better assigned as /fri/ (matching the English source and the spelling) to resolve the homophone, but this is beyond the scope of the current compound review.

### 2. Morphological pattern: Adjective + Verb → Noun
**Severity: Low**
`fri-chuz` follows the pattern Adj + V → N. In VELA, the established compounding patterns are primarily:
- N + N (`lern-hous`)
- N + V → profession (`wok-po`)
- Adj + N (`big-muzik-grup`)

The Adj + V → N pattern is less common but has a precedent in `self-nof` (Pronoun + V → N, approved). The pattern is productive and transparent: "free-choosing" → "the thing that is free choosing" → "free will." No rule blocks it.

**Recommendation:** Accept as valid. Consider documenting Adj+V→N as a recognized compounding pattern in the formation principles (currently implicit).

### 3. Semantic scope: Does `fri-chuz` cover all senses of "free will"?
**Severity: Very Low**
Philosophical "free will" has multiple senses:
1. **Metaphysical:** The capacity of agents to choose between possible futures (libertarian free will)
2. **Compatibilist:** The ability to act according to one's desires without external coercion
3. **Theological:** The capacity to choose good or evil (Augustinian, Molinist)
4. **Legal:** The voluntariness of an act (not under duress)

`fri-chuz` = "free choose" most naturally maps to sense 1 and 2. Sense 4 (legal) might prefer a separate term (`fri-desid`? "free decision"), but this can be proposed later if needed. Sense 3 is culturally specific and can be expressed periphrastically.

**Recommendation:** Accept `fri-chuz` for the primary metaphysical/philosophical sense. No need to overload the term.

### 4. Duplicate entry: `desid` vs. `disaid`
**Severity: N/A (not a compound problem, flagged for awareness)**
The base lexicon contains two entries for "to decide": `desid` (/desid/) and `disaid` (/disaid/). This inconsistency should be resolved in a future cleanup, but it does not affect `fri-chuz`.

## Proposed Alternatives (if MODIFY/REJECT)

### Evaluated and rejected:

| Alternative | Verdict | Reason |
|-------------|---------|--------|
| `freewil` | ❌ Reject | `wil` = future tense suffix (-wil). Structural homonymy: `mi freewil go` = "I will go freely" OR "my free will goes." Fatal ambiguity. |
| `fri-vol` | ❌ Reject | `vol` is not an existing root. Requires a new atom slot for Latin *voluntas*. Violates 200-atom ceiling for marginal gain. |
| `fri-wil` | ❌ Reject | Same structural homonymy as `freewil`. `wil` is a grammatical suffix, not a noun root. |
| `liberum` / `arbitrium` | ❌ Reject | Latin loan atoms. Violate atom ceiling. Opaque to Germanic/Sinitic/Slavic speakers. Would need phonological adaptation that strips recognizability. |
| `fri chuz` (two words) | ❌ Reject | Produces a verb phrase "to freely choose," not the nominal concept "free will." Syntactically and philosophically inadequate (see Compound vs Separate Words above). |
| `fri-desid` | ❌ Reject | "Free decision" — close but semantically narrower than "free will." A *decision* (`desid`) is a specific act; *free will* is the ongoing *capacity*. Also, `desid`/`disaid` duplication in base lexicon creates version ambiguity. |
| `fri-chuz-nes` | ❌ Reject | Overly long (3 morphemes). The `-nes` suffix is unnecessary because `fri-chuz` already functions as a noun. Adding `-nes` would mean "the quality of free choosing" — circular and redundant. |

**Conclusion:** No alternative is superior to `fri-chuz`. The compound is the best available option given confirmed roots, structural constraints, and semantic precision.

## Rationale Summary

`fri-chuz` is recommended for **APPROVAL** on the following grounds:

1. **Zero atom cost.** Both `fri` and `chuz` are confirmed Phase 4 roots. The compound respects the 200-atom ceiling entirely.

2. **Semantic precision.** "Free + choose → free will" is the correct philosophical decomposition. The compound captures the concept's core — the capacity for uncoerced choice — more accurately than "free + will/desire" (English) or "free + arbitration" (Romance), because "choosing" is precisely the faculty under debate.

3. **Universal lexification.** All major philosophical languages have a dedicated term for "free will." VELA's philosophical vocabulary would have a conspicuous gap without one. `fri.dom` = freedom ≠ `fri-chuz` = free will; both are needed.

4. **Compound precedent.** Section 5.4 already compounds abstract philosophical nouns from existing roots: `self-nof` (consciousness), `tru-nes` (truth), `nof-nes` (knowledge). `fri-chuz` follows the same design principle: zero new atoms, maximum transparency.

5. **No false friends.** Exhaustive cross-linguistic check reveals no cognate traps for either root or the compound as a unit.

6. **No viable alternative.** Every alternative either: (a) requires a new atom (`fri-vol`), (b) creates catastrophic structural ambiguity (`freewil` / `fri-wil` — `wil` is the future tense suffix), (c) is semantically narrower (`fri-desid`), or (d) produces a verb phrase instead of a noun (`fri chuz` as two words).

7. **AFI homophone is manageable.** `fri` /frai/ shares its AFI with `frai` ("to fly"), but context makes the compound unambiguous. This is a system-level imperfection that should be resolved in a future AFI audit (recommend `fri` → /fri/), not a compound-level blocker.

**One flag for committee follow-up:** The AFI assignment `/frai/` for `fri` creates a spoken homophone with `frai` ("to fly"). Recommend referring this to the Phonologist for AFI audit in Phase 6. The written forms are distinct and the compound is unambiguous in practice, but the homophone is an avoidable system imperfection.
