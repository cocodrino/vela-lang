# Profession Suffix Evaluation — VELA Aestheticist

## Evaluation Criteria

- **Vowel harmony**: Vowels should coordinate within a word, avoiding clashing heights/backness.
- **Open syllables**: CV structure preferred; closed syllables and consonant clusters degrade singability.
- **Singability**: Can the word hold a melodic line? Does it have sustained vowels?
- **Melodic prosody**: Accent placement and syllable count should create rhythmic elegance.
- **Memorability**: Does the form leave a sonic imprint?

## Candidate Systems

### 1. Suffix `-er`

| Example | Phonetic | Syllables | Accent | Closed? |
|---------|----------|-----------|--------|---------|
| `lern-er` | /LER.ner/ | 2 | first (penult) | `lern` is closed |
| `sik-fix-er` | /SIK.fi.ker/ | 3 | second (middle) | `sik` is closed |
| `food-mak-er` | /FUD.ma.ker/ | 3 | second (middle) | `food` is closed |

**Phonological analysis:**

- `-er` is a single open syllable /er/, which is melodically excellent — the schwa-like /e/ or full /er/ provides a clean, sustained singing vowel.
- The profession words, however, carry closed initial syllables (`lern`, `sik`, `food`, `mak`). In VELA, closed syllables and final clusters like /rn/, /k/+/consonant/ violate the "open syllable" desideratum.
- Accent on the first syllable for `LER-ner` is beautiful — trochaic, falling rhythm, natural for chants. But `sik-FI-ker` and `food-MA-ker` place accent in the middle, creating amphibrach-like tension. Singable, but less grounded.
- The `-er` suffix itself is inherited from English and echoes Latin/Romance `-or` / `-arius`. This gives it a faint colonial flavor — not necessarily bad, but not distinctively VELA.

**Melodic score:** 6/10 — the suffix is lovely, but the roots often force closed syllables or clusters that fight VELA phonotactics.

### 2. Suffix `-po`

| Example | Phonetic | Syllables | Accent | Closed? |
|---------|----------|-----------|--------|---------|
| `leri-po` | /LE.ri.po/ | 3 | first | all open |

**Phonological analysis:**

- All syllables are open. /LE/ — bright, front vowel. /ri/ — high front, light. /po/ — warm, mid-back rounded, resonant. The vowel sequence (/e/ → /i/ → /o/) does not strictly harmonize by height or backness, but the progression creates an arched melodic contour: rise-fall-sustain. This is *singly* beautiful.
- `/rn/` cluster is eliminated by the epenthetic vowel (`leri` from `lern`). This shows `-po` naturally forces root reshaping toward open syllables — a *pro-aesthetic* pressure.
- Accent on first syllable: trochaic + dactylic tendency (`LE-ri-po`). This is the rhythm of nursery rhymes and lullabies. Highly singable.
- The vowel /o/ is the warmest, most resonant of the cardinal vowels. Ending a profession word in /o/ gives it weight and dignity without heaviness.
- `-po` has no obvious Romance or Germanic cognate. It sounds invented — which is a virtue for an engineered language.

**Melodic score:** 9/10 — open, arched, warm, dignified.

### 3. Suffix `-ist` / `-isti`

| Example | Phonetic | Syllables | Accent | Closed? |
|---------|----------|-----------|--------|---------|
| `lern-ist` | /LER.nist/ | 1 (cluster hell) | — | closed cluster |
| `lern-isti` | /LER.nis.ti/ | 3 | first | `nist` is closed |

**Phonological analysis:**

- `lern-ist` is phonotactically catastrophic for VELA: /rnst/ final cluster, three consecutive consonants. Unsingable. Would be rendered `leri-nis-ti` in practice, at which point we have a 4-syllable word.
- `lern-isti` = /LER.nis.ti/: accent on first syllable (good), but `nis` is closed. The /nst/→/nis/ repair is possible but creates an extra syllable.
- The final /i/ is bright and memorable — almost a diminutive or affectionate marker. This is aesthetically coherent (high front vowel = light/bright), but it may feminize or trivialize profession words unintentionally.
- `-isti` echoes Esperanto `-isto`, Italian `-ista`. Familiar to auxlang audiences, but again: derivative.

**Melodic score:** 5/10 — salvageable but inherently bloated; final /i/ is charming but risks semantic overload.

### 4. No Suffix

| Example | Phonetic | Syllables | Accent | Closed? |
|---------|----------|-----------|--------|---------|
| `leri` | /LE.ri/ | 2 | first | all open |

**Phonological analysis:**

- `leri` = /LE.ri/ is phonotactically perfect: two open syllables, trochaic accent, front vowel harmony (/e/ and /i/ both unrounded, /e/ mid /i/ high — compatible).
- The problem is *functional*, not melodic. A profession word that looks identical to a verb root (e.g., `leri` = "to learn" AND "learner") collapses lexical space and burdens context. Poetry might love the ambiguity; legal or technical registers would suffer.
- If VELA resolves this via determiners or compounding (e.g., `te leri` = "the learned one"), this system becomes viable. But the question explicitly asks for *profession suffixes*, so "no suffix" is a non-answer to the prompt.

**Melodic score:** 8/10 — beautiful but functionally incomplete for the requested category.

## Answers to Key Questions

### Q1: Are 3–4 syllables too heavy?

No. The target is not "monosyllabic minimalism" but *melodic simplicity*. A 3-syllable word with open syllables and first-syllable accent (`LE-ri-po`) is easier to sing and remember than a 2-syllable word with a closed final cluster (`lern-er` → phonotactically repaired anyway).

Japanese profession words are often 3–4 syllables (`sensei`, `isha`, `bengoshi`) and feel light because of strict CV structure. VELA should emulate this lightness, not English/Germanic consonant-density.

### Q2: Does `-er` create melodic patterns easy to chant?

Partially. The suffix itself is chantable (`-er`, `-er`, `-er`), but the roots resist. Real VELA outputs would be `leri-er` or `lerner` — and `/rn/` or `/rner/` are not clean. The chant pattern is broken by required phonotactic repairs.

### Q3: Most beautiful profession word in each system?

| Suffix | Most beautiful form | Why |
|--------|---------------------|-----|
| `-er` | `fo-mer` (former?) | Actually, `maker` → `ma-ker` is best: /MA.ker/, open syllables, accent on warm /a/. But this is cherry-picking; most roots worsen. |
| `-po` | `leri-po` | Arched vowel melody /e-i-o/, all open, trochaic-dactylic, ends in warm /o/. Near perfection. |
| `-isti` | `leri-ni-sti` (post-repair) | 4 syllables, accent first, ends in bright /i/. Pretty but long. |
| No suffix | `leri` | Perfect sonority, zero redundancy, but ambiguous. |

## Recommendation

**Use `-po`.**

Justification:

1. **It forces open syllables.** The suffix's vowel-initial quality (`-po`) breaks potential final clusters and encourages roots to resolve to open syllables (`leri` rather than `lern`). This is an *aesthetic guardrail* — it makes VELA more VELA-like.

2. **It has optimal prosody.** First-syllable accent (`LE-ri-po`) creates a falling, chantable rhythm. The three-syllable length gives enough melodic contour for a musical phrase without heaviness.

3. **It ends in /o/.** Warm, resonant, grounded. Profession words name identity — they should feel solid. /o/ provides this in a way that /er/ (neutral) and /i/ (bright/light) do not.

4. **It is distinctive.** `-po` does not map transparently to Esperanto `-isto`, English `-er`, or `-or`. This originality supports VELA's claim to be an engineered aesthetic language, not a relex.

5. **It scales gracefully.** Longer compounds remain singable:
   - `te-ko-wri-po` (writer of technology) = /TE.ko.WRI.po/ — 4 open syllables, accent first, arching vowel melody.

**Counter-argument to address:** Some may claim 3 syllables is too long for "hello"-level vocabulary frequency. Response: VELA profession words are not discourse particles; they occur in deliberate utterances where a touch of melodic weight is appropriate and even desirable. Casual greetings can remain 1–2 syllables. Profession identity deserves 3.

---

## Key Learnings:

1. A good VELA suffix should act as a *phonotactic guardrail*, pushing roots toward open syllables, not merely tagging them after the fact.
2. First-syllable accent + open syllables + 3-syllable length = the rhythmic profile of lullabies and chants, which is VELA's melodic home.
3. The vowel /o/ is underutilized in auxlang suffixes; its warmth and resonance make it ideal for words denoting identity and social role.
4. Derivative suffixes (`-er`, `-ist`, `-ist`) carry cognitive baggage from source languages; an original suffix (`-po`) strengthens VELA's aesthetic independence.
5. What is "too long" depends on syllable structure, not absolute count: `sik-fikser` (3 syllables, 2 closed) feels heavier than `leri-po` (3 syllables, all open).
