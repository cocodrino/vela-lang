## Overall Aesthetic Assessment

This batch suffers from an **"English-pidgin" syndrome**: too many words are English spelled phonetically rather than VELA words born from VELA phonotactics. The worst offenders are monosyllables ending in hard plosives (t, k, d, p) — about a third of the list — which abandon the soft-landing principle that makes VELA a lullaby language.

**Sibilant stacking** (s...s, sh...s) and brutal clusters (sk, skr, st, str, ks, nd, nt, ft) appear far too often.

**The beautiful formula:** Penultimate pitch accent, harmonious vowel contrasts (warm ↔ light), codas that are open vowels or sonorants (l, r, n, m). They feel invented, not borrowed.

**Aesthetic recommendation:** VELA needs a phonotactic edict — final plosives are banned except in special register; words should resolve into vowels or sonorants. Every word must pass the **"cradle test"**: can you sing it to a sleeping child without waking them? If it ends in -t, -k, -d, -p, or carries a cluster like skr-, it fails.

## Key Recommendations
- Add terminal vowels (a, i, o, u) to words ending in plosives
- Re-syllabify compounds for melody (no hyphens in spoken form)
- Ban sk-, skr-, st-, str-, ks-, nd-, nt-, ft- clusters
- Cadence over correctness: slightly less precise but more singable = right choice
