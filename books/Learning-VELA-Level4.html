The one rule: VELA has no passive — it uses “people”

When the doer doesn’t matter, make man-en the subject

English says “VELA is spoken here”. VELA keeps a normal active sentence and just uses man-en (people) as a vague subject.

VELAEnglish
Man-en toka VELA hier.VELA is spoken here.
Man-en mak dis kar in Jerman.This car is made in Germany.
Man-en sel-ed la old hous.The old house was sold.

There is no special “passive” form to learn — the trick is always the same word: man-en.

Dialogue — Hai man-en mak li?

A
Dis breda es veri gud! Wer man-en mak li?
This bread is very good! Where is it made?
B
Man-en mak li in la smol bred-mak-hous.
It’s made in the small bakery.
A
And man-en toka VELA dar?
And is VELA spoken there?
B
Ye! In dis siti man-en toka li in ol ples.
Yes! In this city it’s spoken everywhere.

Your turn

Exercise 1 · make it impersonal

“English is spoken here.” (English = Inglish)

Man-en toka Inglish hier.

“The book was written in the city.”

Man-en rit-ed la buka in la siti.

The one rule: verb + dat + the exact words

VELA does not shift the tense the way English does

English turns “I am tired” into “he said he was tired”. VELA keeps the original tense — just add dat.

VELAEnglish
Li sey: “Mi es tireda.”He says: “I am tired.”
Li tok-ed dat li es tireda.He said that he was tired.
Mi promis-ed dat mi kom-wil.I promised that I would come.

The tense inside stays as the speaker first said it — much simpler than English.

Dialogue — Wat li tok-ed?

A
Wat Ana tok-ed?
What did Ana say?
B
Li tok-ed dat li kom-wil tomoro.
She said that she’ll come tomorrow.
A
And li tal-ed dat li bring-wil la buka-n?
And did she say she’ll bring the books?
B
Ye, li promis-ed dat.
Yes, she promised that.

Your turn

Exercise 1 · report the words

“I am happy.” → He said that…

Li tok-ed dat li es hapi.

“We will work.” → They said that…

De tok-ed dat de werk-wil.

The one rule: weda for yes/no, question words for the rest

The reported question keeps normal word order (not question order)

VELAEnglish
Li ask-ed weda mi kom.He asked whether I was coming.
Li ask-ed wat mi wan.He asked what I wanted.
Mi no nof wer li liv.I don’t know where he lives.

Compare the direct question Wat yu wan? with the reported …wat yu wan — same words, just tucked inside.

Dialogue — Li ask-ed weda…

A
La new man ask-ed weda wi toka Inglish.
The new man asked whether we speak English.
B
And yu tal-ed li wat wi toka?
And did you tell him what we speak?
A
Ye. Li no understanda wer li es!
Yes. He doesn’t understand where he is!
B
Ha! Den wi mas helpa li lern VELA.
Ha! Then we must help him learn VELA.

Your turn

Exercise 1 · report the question

“Do you work here?” → She asked whether…

Li ask-ed weda mi werka hier.

“Where is the school?” → I don’t know…

Mi no nof wer es la lern-hous.

The one rule: a scale of certainty

From “maybe” to “probably” to “should”

VELAEnglish
Mebi li kom.Maybe he comes.
Li es mos-laikli richa.He is probably rich.
Yu shoda rest.You should rest.
Li es posibel.It is possible.

shoda works like the other modals — the verb after it stays short: Yu shoda slip mor.

Dialogue — Wat mi shoda don?

A
Mi es veri tireda ol la taim.
I’m very tired all the time.
B
Mi sugest dat yu shoda slip mor.
I suggest that you should sleep more.
A
Mebi. Mi werka tu meni our-en.
Maybe. I work too many hours.
B
Den yu shoda werka les. Li es posibel!
Then you should work less. It’s possible!

Your turn

Exercise 1 · give advice

“You should eat more.”

Yu shoda eat mor.

“She is probably at home.”

Li es mos-laikli hom-to.

The one rule: three verbs for change & appearance

bikom = be+come, and it means exactly that

VELAEnglish
La child bikom un dokter.The child becomes a doctor.
Li sima veri tireda nau-dei.She seems very tired today.
Wat hapen-ed hier yestdei?What happened here yesterday?

La siti chang mor-mor. Li bikom veri difren from bifor. — The city changes more and more. It becomes very different from before.

Dialogue — La siti chang

A
Dis siti sima veri difren nau!
This city seems very different now!
B
Ye, li bikom big-big. Wat hapen-ed?
Yes, it’s become huge. What happened?
A
Meni new man-en kom-ed. La old strita-n chang-ed.
Many new people came. The old streets changed.
B
Haueva, la herta of la siti es sem.
However, the heart of the city is the same.

The one rule: signal the turn

Where bot is small, haueva is strong

VELAEnglish
Li es smol. Haueva, li es veri strong.He is small. However, he is very strong.
Tuo li es tireda, li werka.Although he is tired, he works.
No go hom; insteda, kom wit mi.Don’t go home; instead, come with me.

haueva usually opens its own sentence; tuo opens the “although” half.

Dialogue — Tuo li es difren…

A
Mi no luva la new plan.
I don’t love the new plan.
B
Tuo li es difren, li es mor guda. Dat es la tru-tin.
Although it’s different, it’s better. That’s the fact.
A
Mebi. Haueva, mi wan mor taim for tinka.
Maybe. However, I want more time to think.
B
Dat es un gud reason. Wi wait, den.
That’s a good reason. We’ll wait, then.

Your turn

Exercise 1 · join with contrast

“The book is old. However, it is good.”

“Although I am tired, I study.”

Tuo mi es tireda, mi stodi.

7 Leson seven

For lern — Cause, purpose & result

give the reason & the goaluse bikos / so / forlink cause to effect

The one rule: because → so, and for for a goal

Three links: reason, result, purpose

VELAEnglish
Mi werka bikos mi wan moni.I work because I want money. (reason)
Mi wan moni, so mi werka.I want money, so I work. (result)
Mi werka for lern.I work (in order) to learn. (purpose)

To say the goal, put for + the short verb: Mi go a la siti for si mi-se fren. — I go to the city to see my friend.

Dialogue — Wai yu lern VELA?

A
Wai yu lern VELA?
Why do you learn VELA?
B
Mi lern li for toka wit new fren-en.
I learn it to speak with new friends.
A
Gud reason! Li es simpli, so man-en lern li fasta.
Good reason! It’s simple, so people learn it fast.
B
Tru! Mi lern li bikos mi luva la wailda.
True! I learn it because I love the world.

Your turn

Exercise 1 · reason, result, purpose

“I sleep because I am tired.”

Mi slipa bikos mi es tireda.

“I go to the shop to buy bread.”

Mi go a la shop for bai breda.

The one rule: open, support, and invite

A clear frame for an opinion

VELAEnglish
In mi-se tink, VELA es importenta.In my view, VELA is important.
Mi agria wit yu, bikos li es simpli.I agree with you, because it’s simple.
Mi difren-tink: li es tu new.I disagree: it’s too new.

Invite the other side: Wat yu tinka? — What do you think?

Dialogue — Wat yu tinka?

A
In mi-se tink, ol man-en shoda lern un langwija.
In my view, everyone should learn a conlang.
B
Mi agria! Li helpa man-en understanda de.
I agree! It helps people understand each other.
A
Haueva, som man-en difren-tink. Wai?
However, some people disagree. Why?
B
De sey dat li es tu hard. Bot dat no es tru.
They say that it’s too hard. But that isn’t true.

The one rule: a scale from “a little” to “too”

Put the degree word before the adjective

VELAEnglish
La kofi es smol-wei hota.The coffee is a little hot.
La kofi es veri hota.The coffee is very hot.
La kofi es tu hota.The coffee is too hot. (more than good)

Fine comparison: mor and mor hota (hotter and hotter), les hota from bifor (less hot than before).

Dialogue — Tu hota!

A
Hai es la kofi?
How’s the coffee?
B
Li es tu hota nau! Mi wait un smol-wei.
It’s too hot now! I’ll wait a little.
A
Mi-se es les hota. Li es veri gud.
Mine is less hot. It’s very good.
B
Den mi drinka mi-se slo-slo.
Then I’ll drink mine slowly.

Your turn

Exercise 1 · pick the degree

“The bag is too heavy.” (heavy = hevi)

La bagi es tu hevi.

“I am a little tired.”

Mi es smol-wei tireda.

11 Leson ten-wan

Read an opinion — Wai un langwija?

read a real argumentfollow the reasonsmeet linking words in use

La tekst

Wai la wailda needa un langwija

Meni man-en tinka dat wi no needa un new langwija. Haueva, mi difren-tink. In mi-se tink, un simpli langwija sem VELA kan helpa la wailda.

Firsta, li es fera. Wen tu man-en toka un langwija, no wan hava mor power from la difren. De es ekwal.

Sekonda, li es fasta. Bikos in VELA ol la rul-en es sem, man-en lern li in fyu monta-n, no meni yeer-en. So mor man-en kan toka wit de.

Som man-en sey dat un langwija no es "real". Bot dat no es la tru-tin: un langwija bikom real wen man-en toka li. If wi toka VELA, den li es real.

Many people think we don’t need a new conlang. However, I disagree. In my view, a simple conlang like VELA can help the world. First, it’s fair: when two people speak a conlang, neither has more power than the other — they are equal. Second, it’s faster: because in VELA all the rules are the same, people learn it in a few months, not many years. So more people can speak with one another. Some people say a conlang isn’t “real”. But that isn’t the fact: a language becomes real when people speak it. If we speak VELA, then it is real.

Your turn

Exercise 1 · follow the argument

1. Wat es la firsta reason?

La firsta reason es dat un langwija es fera — man-en es ekwal.

2. Wen un langwija bikom real?

Un langwija bikom real wen man-en toka li.

12 Leson ten-tu

Debata! — Discuss & conclude

hold a real debateuse all of Level 4write your own opinion

A debate — Siti or lond?

A
Mi tinka dat la siti es mos guda for liv. Man-en hava mor werka dar.
I think the city is best to live in. People have more work there.
B
Mi difren-tink. Tuo la siti hava mor werka, li es tu fasta and tu louuda.
I disagree. Although the city has more work, it’s too fast and too loud.
A
Dat es tru. Haueva, in la lond man-en hava fyu fren-en.
That’s true. However, in the country people have few friends.
B
Mebi. Bot mi luva la kalm. In mi-se tink, la lond big-mak la herta.
Maybe. But I love the calm. In my view, the country grows the heart.
A
So wi agria dat wi difren-tink! Bot wan tru-tin: la tu hava biju-nes.
So we agree to disagree! But one fact: both have beauty.
