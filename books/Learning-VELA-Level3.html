The one rule: the question word does double duty

Put the matching question word right after the noun

To glue a whole sentence onto a noun, use the question word that fits: hu for people, wat for things, wer for places. The clause stays in normal order.

VELAEnglish
la person hu liv hierthe man who lives here
la buka wat mi ridathe book that I read
la ples wer wi werkathe place where we work

Position tells you it’s not a question: Hu liv hier? (a question) vs. la person hu liv hier (a description).

Dialogue — La fren hu mi luva

A
Hu es la person wat yu si-ed?
Who is the man that you saw?
B
Li es la fren hu liv nera la river.
He’s the friend who lives near the river.
A
Ah, la person hu werka in la lern-hous?
Ah, the man who works at the school?
B
Ye! Li es la fren hu mi luva mos.
Yes! He’s the friend whom I love the most.

Your turn

Exercise 2 · describe a place

Describe your home starting La hous wer mi liv…

e.g. La hous wer mi liv es smol bot veri nais.

The one rule: if opens, den answers

Real condition = present · unreal = past in both halves

VELAEnglish
If yu werka, den yu hava moni.If you work, then you have money.
If la rein kom, den la plant-en grota.If the rain comes, the plants grow.
If mi es-ed richa, den mi bai-ed un biga hous.If I were rich, I would buy a big house.

For the imagined “would”, VELA just uses the past in both halves — the if makes it clear it isn’t real.

Dialogue — If mi es-ed richa…

A
If yu es-ed richa, wat yu bai-ed?
If you were rich, what would you buy?
B
Mi bai-ed un hous nera la si. And yu?
I’d buy a house near the sea. And you?
A
If mi hava-ed moni, den mi helpa-ed meni pipol.
If I had money, I’d help many people.
B
Dat es un nais drim!
That’s a beautiful dream!

Exercise 2 · your “what if”

The big idea: -mak = “to make (something) ___”

Adjective + -mak becomes a verb of causing

You already know maka = to make. As a small ending, -mak turns a quality into an action.

Adjective+ -makMeaning
strongstrong-makto strengthen
bigbig-makto enlarge
badbad-makto worsen / harm

La son big-mak la plant-en. — The sun makes the plants grow. Tenses work normally: strong-mak-ed (strengthened).

Dialogue — Strong-mak la bodi

A
Mi wan strong-mak mi-se bodi.
I want to strengthen my body.
B
Den ran ol la dei-n! Li strong-mak la herta.
Then run every day! It strengthens the heart.
A
And no bad-mak li wit bada fuda.
And don’t harm it with bad food.
B
Tru-tru! Gud fuda big-mak la strong-nes.
Truly! Good food grows your strength.

Your turn

2. “to make clean” (clean = clean)

Exercise 2 · use it in a sentence

Say “The sun strengthens the tree.”

The big idea: -wei = “in a ___ way”

Root + -wei builds an adverb (a “how” word)

wei means way/path. Add it to a quality or direction and you say how something happens.

VELAEnglish
Li tok soft-wei.She speaks gently.
La bird flai ap-wei.The bird flies upward.
Mi tinka dip-wei.I think deeply.

The adverb sits after the verb, like in English.

Dialogue — Tok soft-wei

A
La child slipa. Plise tok soft-wei.
The child is sleeping. Please speak gently.
B
Sori! Mi luva la wei li slipa.
Sorry! I love the way she sleeps.
A
Ye. Li grota big-wei dis jir.
Yes. She has grown a lot this year.
B
Taim go fasta, no?
Time goes fast, doesn’t it?

Your turn

Exercise 2 · how do you do it?

The big idea: doubling turns up the volume

Repeat a word to intensify it or show growth

Where Level 1 used veri for “very”, doubling goes further — to the extreme, or to a rising change.

BaseDoubledMeaning

La siti grota mor-mor. — The city grows more and more. · Tru-tru, li es un guda fren. — Truly, he is a good friend.

6 Leson siks

Word order & the case markers

keep S-V-O clearfront for emphasis with -achain -se and -to

The idea: VELA marks a word only when order isn’t enough

Normally the position does the work

In Mi si la doga, mi is the doer (before the verb) and la doga is the target (after). No endings needed — that is the whole point of SVO.

VELAEnglish
La person si la kata.The man sees the cat.
La kata-a, la person si.The cat, the man sees. (object moved to front → mark it with -a)

Only when you move the object to the front do you add -a, so the listener still knows who does what.

Chaining owners (-se) and places (-to)

VELAEnglish
la person-se doga-se neimthe man’s dog’s name
Mi liv la biga siti-to.I live in the big city.

Dialogue — Hu-se buka?

A
Dis buka — hu-se es li?
This book — whose is it?
B
Li es mi-se broter-se fren-se buka!
It’s my brother’s friend’s book!
A
Ha! And la buka-a, hu rida?
Ha! And the book — who reads it?
B
Ol wi rida li, in la hous-to.
All of us read it, at home.

The big idea: read a compound left to right

Chain roots with a hyphen — up to three

VELA builds most “new” things from old roots. Read each piece and the meaning appears.

CompoundPiecesMeaning
far-tok-boxfar + talk + boxtelephone

The rule: at most three roots. If you need more, break it into two words.

The one rule: a question + plise = a polite request

Turn an order into a request with kan and plise

DirectPolite
Giva mi la watre. (Give me the water.)Yu kan giva mi la watre, plise? (Could you give me the water, please?)
Helpa mi. (Help me.)Yu kan helpa mi, plise? (Could you help me, please?)

Say sorry with Mi es sori, thank with Mi tanki yu (or just Tanki!).

Dialogue — In la shop

A
Helo! Yu kan giva mi tu breda, plise?
Hello! Could you give me two loaves, please?
B
Sori, wi hava wan breda nau.
Sorry, we have one loaf now.
A
No problem. Wan es gud. Tanki yu!
No problem. One is fine. Thank you!
B
Tanki yu! Gud dei!
Thank you! Good day!

La poema

Son and Moon

La son shin ovar la mautin,
la river ran slo-slo daun.
La flauor-en open a la lait,
and ol la lond es warma and brite.

Wen la noita kom, la moon rais,
la star-en shin, smol-smol and hai.
La wind tok soft-wei tru la tre-n,
and ol la wailda slipa tru-tru.

Line by line: The sun shines over the mountain, / the river runs slowly down. / The flowers open to the light, / and all the land is warm and bright. // When the night comes, the moon rises, / the stars shine, tiny and high. / The wind speaks gently through the trees, / and all the world sleeps, truly.

12 Leson ten-tu

Read a story — La tri child-en

read a whole folktaleuse everythingtell your own

La stori

La tri child-en and la old hous

Wan taim, tri child-en hu liv-ed nera un biga fores luz-ed la wei hom. La noita kom-ed slo-slo, and la wind tok-ed strong-wei tru la tre-n. La child-en es-ed afreda.

"If wi si un hous," tok-ed la firsta child, "den wi es safi." So de woka-ed mor-mor, and sun de si-ed un smol lait. Li es-ed un olda hous wer un warm-hert wuman liv-ed.

La wuman hu open-ed la dor giva-ed de warma supa and un ples to slipa. "No person luza la wei foreva," li tok-ed soft-wei. "La moring big-mak la hop."

In la moring, la son shin-ed brite, and la child-en si-ed la wei hom. De no forget-ed la wuman hu helpa-ed de — de dip-luva-ed li for ol la jir-en.

Once, three children who lived near a big forest lost the way home. Night came slowly, and the wind spoke strongly through the trees. The children were afraid. “If we find a house,” said the first child, “then we are safe.” So they walked more and more, and soon they saw a small light. It was an old house where a kind woman lived. The woman who opened the door gave them warm soup and a place to sleep. “No one loses the way forever,” she said gently. “The morning grows hope.” In the morning, the sun shone bright, and the children found the way home. They did not forget the woman who helped them — they cherished her for all the years.
