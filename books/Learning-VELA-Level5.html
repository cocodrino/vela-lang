The big idea: a grid you never memorise

Pick a quantifier + a base — that’s the word

{som · ol · no · eni} + {person · tin · ples}. You already know every piece; just join them.

persontin (thing)ples (place)
somsom-person (someone)som-tin (something)som-ples (somewhere)
olol-person (everyone)ol-tin (everything)ol-ples (everywhere)
nono-person (nobody)no-tin (nothing)no-ples (nowhere)
enieni-person (anyone)eni-tin (anything)eni-ples (anywhere)

“Each other” = double the pronoun: Wi help wi-wi. — We help each other. · De luva de-de. — They love each other.

Dialogue — Ol-person nof

A
Som-person tok-ed dat yu go-wil. Es li tru?
Someone said you’ll leave. Is it true?
B
Ha! No-tin es sikreta hier. Ol-person nof ol-tin.
Ha! Nothing is secret here. Everyone knows everything.
A
Tru-tru. Bot wi trasta wi-wi, no?
True. But we trasta each other, right?
B
Ol-tu of wi, ye. Mi go no-ples if yu no es hier.
Both of us, yes. I go nowhere without you.

2 Leson tu

Maka eni wod — Coining words on the fly

never be stuck for a wordbuild it from rootsthe master’s trick

The mastery move: you don’t look words up — you make them

Four endings turn any root into what you need

EndingTurns a root into…Example
-maka verb of causingstrong-mak = to strengthen
-weian adverb (how)soft-wei = gently
-nesan abstract nounbiju-nes = beauty
-poa person / rolelern-po = teacher

No word for “to deepen”? Build it: dip-mak. No word for “deeply”? dip-wei. This is the heart of VELA — a small set of roots, endless words.

Dialogue — Mi no nof la wod!

A
Hai mi sey “to make clean”? Mi no nof la wod.
How do I say “to make clean”? I don’t know the word.
B
Yu no needa nof li — maka li! Clean + mak = clean-mak.
You don’t need to know it — build it! Clean + make = clean-mak.
A
Ah! So “quickly” es fast-wei, and “weakness” es wik-nes?
Ah! So “quickly” is fast-wei, and “weakness” is wik-nes?
B
Tru-tru! Nau yu tinka sem un VELA-person.
Exactly! Now you think like a VELA speaker.

4 Leson kwatro

Register — Formal & casual

read the roomsoften or sharpensound right anywhere

The one idea: same grammar, different warmth

VELA has no “formal endings” — you shift with word choice

Casual / bluntWarm / polite
Giva mi la salt. (Give me the salt.)Yu kan giva mi la salt, plise?
No!Mi es sori, bot no.
Yu es rong. (You’re wrong.)Mi tinka dat li es un smol difren.

Soften with mebi, mi tinka dat, smol-wei; add warmth with plise and tanki.

Dialogue — Tu weis to sey “no”

A
Yu kan werka dis noita?
Can you work tonight?
B
Mi es sori, bot mi no kan — mi-se famili wait.
I’m sorry, but I can’t — my family is waiting.
A
Mi understanda. Mebi tomoro, den?
I understand. Maybe tomorrow, then?
B
Ye, wit joi. Tanki for understanda.
Yes, gladly. Thanks for understanding.

Exercise 1 · soften it

Make Kom hier. (Come here.) polite.

5 Leson faiv

Emphasis — Moving words for effect

stress what mattersfront for dramaspeak with rhythm

The one rule: normal is SVO — move a word to spotlight it

Front the object (mark it with -a) to put it in the spotlight

VELAEnglish
Mi luva yu.I love you. (neutral)
Yu-a, mi luva.You — you are the one I love.
Dis buka-a, ol-person rida.This book, everyone reads.

This is literary/emphatic (grammar §1.2). In plain speech, keep SVO — but a master knows when to break the line for effect.

Dialogue — Yu-a!

A
Meni pipol help-ed mi. Bot yu-a, mi no forget-wil.
Many people helped me. But you — you I won’t forget.
B
Dat-a, mi luva to her.
That — that I love to hear.
A
Wi-wi, wi understanda.
We understand each other.
B
Ol otra tin, mi kan luz. Bot no dis.
Everything else I can lose. But not this.

Your turn

Exercise 1 · front for emphasis

Spotlight “this song”: “Everyone loves this song.”

Dis sonja-a, ol-person luva.

6 Leson siks

Nuance — Fine shades of meaning

big vs huge vs vastchoose the exact wordcolour your speech

The master picks the exact shade

Three tools for degree & nuance

ToolExampleShade
degree wordveri bigavery big
reduplicationbig-bighuge / vast (extreme)
compoundwarm-hert vs gudakind-hearted vs just “good”

Same idea, sharper: tireda (tired) → veri tireda → dip-tireda (deeply tired / exhausted). The compound carries the nuance.

Dialogue — No “big”, bot “big-big”

A
La mar es biga.
The sea is big.
B
Big-big! “Biga” es tu smol un wod for li.
Vast! “Big” is too small a word for it.
A
Tru. And mi es no “tireda” — mi es dip-tireda.
True. And I’m not “tired” — I’m exhausted.
B
Den rest! La wod chuza la fel-nes.
Then rest! The word chooses the feeling.

Make “small” into “tiny”.

smol-smol

Say “deeply happy”.

Wai mi luva mi-se siti

Mi luva mi-se siti for meni reason. Firsta, la pipol es warm-hert. Sekonda, ol-ples yu go, yu si nais olda hous-en. Haueva, li no es ol-gud: la strita-n es tu loud. For eksampel, in moring li es hard to slipa. Bot in la end, la guda es mor biga from la bada.

I love my city for many reasons. First, the people are kind. Second, everywhere you go, you see beautiful old houses. However, it isn’t perfect: the streets are too loud. For example, in the morning it’s hard to sleep. But in the end, the good is bigger than the bad.

La poema — La river of taim

La river of taim

La taim ran sem un kwaiat river,
slo-slo, tru la grin lond.
Wi ol es smol boota-n on li,
and no-person nof wer li go.

So luva ol-person nau, nau,
bikos la watre no kom bak.
Evri dei es un nais giv,
and evri noita, un kwaiat sonja.

Time runs like a quiet river, / slowly, through the green land. / We are all small boats on it, / and no one knows where it goes. // So love everyone now, now, / because the water doesn’t come back. / Every day is a beautiful gift, / and every night, a quiet song.

When there’s no direct word, build or rephrase

11 Leson ten-wan

Read an essay — La power of un simpli langwija

La tekst

La power of un simpli langwija

Meni pipol tinka dat un langwija mas es hard to es "real". Haueva, mi bilever dat la tru-tin es la otra wei: la mos strong langwija es la mos simpli.

Firsta, un simpli langwija es fera. Wen no-person hava mor power from la wod-en, ol-person es ekwal. Sekonda, li es fasta: bikos ol la rul-en es sem, eni-person kan lern li in fyu monta-n.

Som pipol sey dat simpli min "smol" or "poor". Bot dat es rong. Un simpli langwija kan sey ol-tin — luva, dipa-afreda, biju-nes, dip-nof — bikos li maka wod-en from wod-en. In la end, la power no es in meni rul-en, bot in la fri to maka.

Many people think a language must be hard to be “real”. However, I believe the fact is the other way: the strongest language is the simplest. First, a simple language is fair. When no one has more power from the words, everyone speaks as equals. Second, it’s faster: because all the rules are the same, anyone can learn it in a few months. Some people say simple means “small” or “poor”. But that’s wrong. A simple language can say everything — love, dread, beauty, wisdom — because it builds words from words. In the end, the power isn’t in many rules, but in the freedom to build.

Your turn

Exercise 1 · follow the argument

1. Wat es la tu reason for un simpli langwija?

Li es fera (ol-person es ekwal) and li es fasta (eni-person lern li kwikli).

2. Wer es la power, in la end?

La power es in la fri to maka wod-en from wod-en.
