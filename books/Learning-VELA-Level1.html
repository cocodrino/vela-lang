The one rule: es = am / is / are

One word for “to be” — and it never changes

In English you juggle am, is, are. In VELA there is just es, the same for everyone. The order is Subject — es — the rest.

VELAEnglish
Mi es hapi.I am happy.
Yu es mi fren.You are my friend.
Li es guda.He / she is good.

To make it negative, put no before it: Mi no es sori. — I am not sorry.

Dialogue — Tu fren

A
Helo! Mi neim es Ana.
Hello! My name is Ana.
B
Helo, Ana! Mi es Ben.
Hello, Ana! I am Ben.
A
Yu es mi fren. Mi es hapi!
You are my friend. I am happy!
B
Mi es hapi tu!
I am happy too!

The one rule: la & un

“the” and “a” — that’s all the articles there are

la means the (a specific one). un means a / an (any one). They never change for gender or number.

VELAEnglish
la moterthe mother
un frena friend
un big familia big family

Adjectives go before the noun: un smol childa — a small child. la old man — the old man.

One tiny sound-rule: big vs biga

The dictionary gives adjectives with a final vowel: biga (big), guda (good), olda (old). Right before a noun, drop that vowel — the noun cushions the sound. On its own after es, keep it.

Before a noun (short)Alone after es (full)
un big familiLa famili es biga.
la old manLa man es olda.

Words that already end in a vowel or a soft sound never change: smol, yong, hapi stay the same everywhere.

Dialogue — Mi famili

A
Dis es mi famili. Mi hava un moter and un fater.
This is my family. I have a mother and a father.
B
La moter es yong. La fater es olda!
The mother is young. The father is old!
A
Ha! Mi hava un smol broter tu.
Ha! I have a small brother too.
B
Un big famili. Nais!
A big family. Nice!

The one rule: the present is the dictionary word

No endings for “I / you / he” — the verb never changes for the person

To speak in the present, use the verb exactly as the dictionary gives it. The word order is Subject — verb — object, just like English.

VELAEnglish
Mi eata fooda.I eat food.
Yu drinka watre.You drink water.
Li rida un buka.He / she reads a book.
Wi si la doga.We see the dog.

Same verb for everyone: mi eata, yu eata, li eata, wi eata, de eata. To say no, put no first: Mi no eata miten. — I don’t eat meat.

Dialogue — Wat yu wan?

A
Mi es hagri. Mi wan fooda!
I am hungry. I want food!
B
Mi maka fooda nau. Yu drinka watre?
I’m making food now. Do you drink water?
A
Ye, plise. And la doga wan fooda alo!
Yes, please. And the dog wants food too!
B
Ha! Mi si la doga. Li es hagri alo.
Ha! I see the dog. He is hungry too.

Exercise 2 · make it negative

Colours: short before a noun, full on their own

Before a nounAlone after es

The one rule: numbers & quantity words go before the noun

Counting things

Put the number first, then the plural noun: tri buka-n (three books), faiva kata-n (five cats). With wan (one) the noun stays singular: wan buka.

VELAEnglish
meni fren-enmany friends

fyu doga-nfew dogs
ol la man-enall the people

Bigger numbers just stack: ten-wan = 11, tu-ten = 20, tu-ten-wan = 21.

The one rule: two endings do all the time-travel

Present = the plain word · Past = -ed · Future = -wil

Past and future attach to the short root (the same short form you saw inside compounds). If the dictionary word ends in a helper vowel, drop it first: eata → eat-ed, maka → mak-ed.

PresentPast (-ed)Future (-wil)
mi si (I see)mi si-ed (I saw)mi si-wil (I’ll see)
mi eata (I eat)mi eat-ed (I ate)mi eat-wil (I’ll eat)
mi go (I go)mi go-ed (I went)mi go-wil (I’ll go)

No irregular verbs — ever. “Was / went / ate / saw” are all just -ed.

Dialogue — Yestdei and tomoro

A
Yestdei mi go-ed a la siti.
Yesterday I went to the city.
B
Nais! Wat yu si-ed dar?
Nice! What did you see there?
A
Mi si-ed meni man-en. Tomoro mi go-wil agen.
I saw many people. Tomorrow I’ll go again.
B
Den mi go-wil wit yu!
Then I’ll go with you!

Your turn

Exercise 1 · change the tense

1. Mi eata fooda. → yesterday

2. Wi si la doga. → tomorrow

Tomoro wi si-wil la doga.
Exercise 2 · translate to VELA

I was happy. (remember: “to be” = es, past = es-ed)

Mi es-ed hapi.

The one rule: the place-ending -to

Add -to to a place to mean “in / at” it

The doer and the thing keep no ending — only the place takes -to.

VELAEnglish
Mi liv siti-to.I live in the city.
Wi woka lern-hous-to.We work at school.
La kata es rum-to.The cat is in the room.

For simple “here / there”, no ending is needed: Mi es hier. — I am here. · La doga es dar. — The dog is there.

Dialogue — Wer es la kata?

A
Wer es la kata? Mi no si li.
Where is the cat? I don’t see it.
B
Li es rum-to. On la beda!
It’s in the room. On the bed!
A
Ha, mi si li nau. La doga es dar alo.
Ha, I see it now. The dog is there too.
B
Tu animal-en in wan rum!
Two animals in one room!

The one rule: veri makes a feeling stronger

Put veri before the adjective

Remember Lesson 2: after es, the adjective is in its full form. So a hard-ending word keeps its vowel: hota, guda.

VELAEnglish
Mi es hapi.I am happy.
Mi es veri hapi.I am very happy.
Li es veri tireda.He is very tired.

Give an opinion with tinka (think): Mi tinka la buka es guda. — I think the book is good.

Dialogue — Hai yu fel?

A
Hai yu fel nau-dei?

B

I’m very tired, but happy. I worked all day.
A
Mi tinka yu needa slipa!
I think you need to sleep!
B
Ha, tru. Mi luva mi-se beda.
Ha, true. I love my bed.
