# STORIES_SET_01

## Story 1: Smol Flai, Big Hart
**VELA:** Yestdei morne, wan smol flai liv-ed in olda hous gardan. Li si-ed biga bird-en flai-ed hai and fel-ed sada bikos li wing no strong-ed. Li jump-ed from flauor tu flauor bot fail-ed agen and agen. Den olda bi kom-ed and tok-ed, "Yu starta smol, bot yu kontinyu." Flai her-ed and train-ed evri day. Li run-ed on folia, jump-ed from ston, and turn-ed in winda. At lasta evning, rain stop-ed and sun kom-ed auta. Flai jump-ed wan taim moa, wing open-ed wide, and li flai-ed ova reda flauor. Gardan animal-en cheer-ed, and smol flai smile-ed wit hapi herta.
**English:** Yesterday morning, a small flai lived in an old house garden. It saw big birds flying high and felt sad because its wings were not strong. It jumped from flower to flower but failed again and again. Then an old bee came and said, "You start small, but you continue." The flai listened and trained every day. It ran on leaves, jumped from stones, and turned in the wind. At last, one evening, rain stopped and sun came out. The flai jumped one more time, opened its wings wide, and flew over a red flower. The garden animals cheered, and the small flai smiled with a happy heart.

## Story 2: Rivr Ston Sonj
**VELA:** In noita bifor storma, wan childa walk-ed wit moter neer river. Childa katch-ed wan smol ston and tink-ed li no importenta. Li trou-ed ston in watre and si-ed ring-en go-ed aronda aronda. Moter tok-ed, "Evri smol akta chang-ed biga wailda." Folo-wan day, childa kom-ed alon and bring-ed tri ston-en: wan reda, wan gray, wan blaka. Li put-ed dem on banka in long line for fisha and birda tu si. Winda kom-ed softa, watre tuch-ed ston-en, and childa her-ed liki smol sonja from river. Li smile-ed and nou-ed dat smol handa can make peis in natur.
**English:** On the night before a storm, a child walked with their mother near the river. The child caught a small stone and thought it was not important. They threw it in the water and saw rings move around and around. The mother said, "Every small act changes the big world." The next day, the child came alone and brought three stones: one red, one gray, one black. They put them on the bank in a long line for fish and birds to see. Soft wind came, water touched the stones, and the child heard something like a small song from the river. They smiled and knew a small hand could make peace in nature.

## Story 3: Dog and Moon
**VELA:** Wan olda doga liv-ed wit wan man in smol londa hous. Evri noita doga sit-ed at dor and si-ed moon. Li tink-ed moon far and kol, and li no trast-ed darka skai. Wan noita, storma kom-ed kwikli, letning flash-ed, and doga shak-ed wit afreda. Man open-ed dor and sit-ed wit doga on flor. Li tok-ed, "Si moon yeta de, baki-wei wo and rein." Doga put-ed hed on man lega and wait-ed. Aft long taim, storma pas-ed, skai open-ed, and moon shin-ed brite agen. Doga stand-ed, wag-ed tail, and bark-ed wan softa sonja. From dat noita, doga no afred-ed darka; li remember-ed moon waita oldei.
**English:** An old dog lived with a man in a small country house. Every night the dog sat at the door and looked at the moon. It thought the moon was far and cold, and it did not trust the dark sky. One night, a storm came quickly, lightning flashed, and the dog shook with fear. The man opened the door and sat with the dog on the floor. He said, "See, the moon is still there, behind clouds and rain." The dog put its head on the man’s leg and waited. After a long time, the storm passed, the sky opened, and the moon shone brightly again. The dog stood, wagged its tail, and barked a soft song. From that night, it no longer feared darkness.

## Story 4: Bread for Fren
**VELA:** Tu fren-en, wan wuman and wan olda man, wok-ed in marketa day. Wuman buy-ed breda for hous and pass-ed narrow strita. Li si-ed olda man sit-ed neer wal, tireda and hungri. Li stop-ed, bot tink-ed, "Mi childa wait-ed for dis breda." Winda turn-ed kol, and olda man kof-ed softa. Wuman brak-ed breda in tu part-en. Li giv-ed biga part to olda man and keep-ed smol part for hous. Olda man eat-ed slow and tok-ed, "Yu handa warm-ed mi day." Wuman go-ed hom wit les breda, bot wen li kom-ed, childa hug-ed li and tok-ed, "Yu face brite liki sun." Li nou-ed kainda feed-ed moa dan mous.
**English:** Two friends, a woman and an old man, worked on market day. The woman bought bread for home and walked through a narrow street. She saw an old man sitting near a wall, tired and hungry. She stopped, but thought, "My child waits for this bread." The wind turned cold, and the old man coughed softly. The woman broke the bread into two parts. She gave the bigger part to the old man and kept a small part for home. The old man ate slowly and said, "Your hand warmed my day." The woman went home with less bread, but when she arrived, her child hugged her and said, "Your face is bright like sun." She knew kindness fed more than the mouth.

## Story 5: Fores Lamp
**VELA:** In dipa fores, tri child-en lose-ed wei bifor noita. De call-ed for helpa, bot onli aul vois answer-ed from hai tre. Child-en walk-ed slow, handa wit handa, and rain start-ed. Wan childa krei-ed, wan childa angri-ed, wan childa quiet-ed. Den de si-ed wan olda hous wit smol windo lait. Wan granmoter open-ed dor and bring-ed dem insaida. Li drai-ed dem wit softa klota, giv-ed hota supa, and tok-ed, "Noita long, bot morne kom." Child-en slip-ed in warma rum. At morne sun, granmoter show-ed safe wei tu londa rodi. Child-en turn-ed and tok-ed, "Yu hous laita save-ed wi herta." Granmoter smile-ed and wave-ed bifor de run-ed hom.
**English:** In a deep forest, three children lost the path before night. They called for help, but only an owl voice answered from a high tree. The children walked slowly, hand in hand, and rain began. One child cried, one got angry, one became quiet. Then they saw an old house with a small window light. A grandmother opened the door and brought them inside. She dried them with soft cloth, gave them hot soup, and said, "Night is long, but morning comes." The children slept in a warm room. At sunrise, the grandmother showed them a safe path to the country road. The children turned and said, "Your house light saved our hearts." The grandmother smiled and waved before they ran home.
