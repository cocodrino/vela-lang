# STORIES_SET_01

## Story 1: Smol Flai, Big Hart
**VELA:** Yest morne, wan smol flai liv-ed in old hous gardan. Li si-ed big bird-en flai-ed high and feel-ed sad bikos li wing no strong-ed. Li jump-ed from flauor tu flauor bot fail-ed agen and agen. Den old bi kom-ed and tok-ed, "Yu start-a smol, bot yu kontinyu-a." Flai hier-ed and train-ed evri day. Li run-ed on leaf, jump-ed from ston, and turn-ed in wind. At last evning, rain stop-ed and sun kom-ed aut. Flai jump-ed wan taim moa, wing open-ed wide, and li flai-ed ova red flauor. Gardan animal-en cheer-ed, and smol flai smile-ed with hapi hart.
**English:** Yesterday morning, a small fly lived in an old house garden. It saw big birds flying high and felt sad because its wings were not strong. It jumped from flower to flower but failed again and again. Then an old bee came and said, "You start small, but you continue." The fly listened and trained every day. It ran on leaves, jumped from stones, and turned in the wind. At last, one evening, rain stopped and sun came out. The fly jumped one more time, opened its wings wide, and flew over a red flower. The garden animals cheered, and the small fly smiled with a happy heart.

## Story 2: Rivr Ston Sonj
**VELA:** In nait bifor storm, wan child walk-ed wit mat neer rivr. Child katch-ed wan smol ston and tink-ed it no importent. Li trou-ed ston in watre and si-ed ring-en go-ed arond arond. Mat tok-ed, "Evri smol act chang-ed big world." Nex day, child kom-ed alon and bring-ed tri ston-en: wan red, wan gray, wan blak. Li put-ed dem on bank in long line for fish and bird tu si. Wind kom-ed soft, watre touch-ed ston-en, and child hier-ed laik smol sonj from rivr. Li smile-ed and nou-ed dat smol hand can make peis in natur.
**English:** On the night before a storm, a child walked with their mother near the river. The child caught a small stone and thought it was not important. They threw it in the water and saw rings move around and around. The mother said, "Every small act changes the big world." The next day, the child came alone and brought three stones: one red, one gray, one black. They put them on the bank in a long line for fish and birds to see. Soft wind came, water touched the stones, and the child heard something like a small song from the river. They smiled and knew a small hand could make peace in nature.

## Story 3: Dog and Moon
**VELA:** Wan old dog liv-ed wit wan man in smol lond hous. Evri nait dog sit-ed at dor and si-ed moon. Li tink-ed moon far and cold, and li no trust-ed dark skai. Wan nait, storm kom-ed quick, letning flash-ed, and dog shak-ed wit fraid. Man open-ed dor and sit-ed wit dog on flor. Li tok-ed, "Si moon still de, bihaind wo and rein." Dog put-ed hed on man leg and wait-ed. Aft long taim, storm pas-ed, skai open-ed, and moon shin-ed bright agen. Dog stand-ed, wag-ed tail, and bark-ed wan soft sonj. From dat nait, dog no fraid-ed dark; li remember-ed moon wait-a always.
**English:** An old dog lived with a man in a small country house. Every night the dog sat at the door and looked at the moon. It thought the moon was far and cold, and it did not trust the dark sky. One night, a storm came quickly, lightning flashed, and the dog shook with fear. The man opened the door and sat with the dog on the floor. He said, "See, the moon is still there, behind clouds and rain." The dog put its head on the man’s leg and waited. After a long time, the storm passed, the sky opened, and the moon shone brightly again. The dog stood, wagged its tail, and barked a soft song. From that night, it no longer feared darkness.

## Story 4: Bread for Fren
**VELA:** Tu fren-en, wan wuman and wan old man, wok-ed in market day. Wuman buy-ed bred for hous and pass-ed narrow strit. Li si-ed old man sit-ed neer wall, tird and hungri. Li stop-ed, bot tink-ed, "Mi child wait-ed for dis bred." Wind turn-ed kold, and old man kough-ed soft. Wuman break-ed bred in tu part-en. Li giv-ed big part to old man and keep-ed smol part for hous. Old man eat-ed slow and tok-ed, "Yu hand warm-ed mi day." Wuman go-ed hom wit less bred, bot wen li kom-ed, child hug-ed li and tok-ed, "Yu face bright laik sun." Li nou-ed kindness feed-ed moa dan maut.
**English:** Two friends, a woman and an old man, worked on market day. The woman bought bread for home and walked through a narrow street. She saw an old man sitting near a wall, tired and hungry. She stopped, but thought, "My child waits for this bread." The wind turned cold, and the old man coughed softly. The woman broke the bread into two parts. She gave the bigger part to the old man and kept a small part for home. The old man ate slowly and said, "Your hand warmed my day." The woman went home with less bread, but when she arrived, her child hugged her and said, "Your face is bright like sun." She knew kindness fed more than the mouth.

## Story 5: Fores Lamp
**VELA:** In deep fores, tri child-en lose-ed path bifor nait. De call-ed for help, bot only owl voys answer-ed from high tre. Child-en walk-ed slow, hand wit hand, and rain start-ed. Wan child krei-ed, wan child angri-ed, wan child quiet-ed. Den de si-ed wan old hous wit smol windo lait. Wan granmat open-ed dor and bring-ed dem insaid. Li dry-ed dem wit soft kloth, giv-ed hot sup, and tok-ed, "Nait long, bot morne kom-a." Child-en slip-ed in warm rum. At morne sun, granmat show-ed safe path tu lond road. Child-en turn-ed and tok-ed, "Yu hous light save-ed wi hart." Granmat smile-ed and wave-ed bifor de run-ed hom.
**English:** In a deep forest, three children lost the path before night. They called for help, but only an owl voice answered from a high tree. The children walked slowly, hand in hand, and rain began. One child cried, one got angry, one became quiet. Then they saw an old house with a small window light. A grandmother opened the door and brought them inside. She dried them with soft cloth, gave them hot soup, and said, "Night is long, but morning comes." The children slept in a warm room. At sunrise, the grandmother showed them a safe path to the country road. The children turned and said, "Your house light saved our hearts." The grandmother smiled and waved before they ran home.
