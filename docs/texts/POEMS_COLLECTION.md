## Poem 1: La Rein and La Longin (Rain and Longing)

**VELA:**
Rein on mi rofa in noita gray,
mi her watre toka in dark skai.
Mi ai si yu memori in wind,
mi herta fel longin and sad.
Wind go aronda mi smol rum slow,
star waita bifor kraud autsida.
Mi tinka yu kom-wil from far lond,
bot dor stei klos in kol.
Wen morne starta wit pale sun,
mi yeta waita, and rein sing.

**English:**
Rain falls on my roof in a gray night,
I hear water speaking in the dark sky.
My eyes see your memory in the wind,
my heart feels longing and sadness.
Wind moves around my small room slowly,
stars wait behind clouds outside.
I think you will come from a far land,
but the door stays closed in the cold.
When morning starts with a pale sun,
I still wait, and rain sings.

**New word:** `longin` (longing) — English root, (C)V.(C)V, vowel-final.

## Poem 2: La Child and La Dream (A Child's Dream)

**VELA:**
Child slipa in beda at morne laita,
li hand holda smol wud horsa.
Li drim skai blu and star near,
bird dansi aronda li hed.
Li go ap mautin wit hapi leg,
sun smile on li ai brite.
Li toka wit moon abau fri lifa,
and moon toka tru-nes in soft wind.
Wen moter kom and toka "start,"
child weika, bot drim stei warm.

**English:**
A child sleeps in bed in morning light,
their hand holds a small wooden horse.
They dream of a blue sky and near stars,
birds dance around their head.
They climb a mountain with happy legs,
the sun smiles on their bright eyes.
They speak with the moon about a free life,
and the moon speaks truth in a soft wind.
When mother comes and says "start,"
the child wakes, but the dream stays warm.

**New words:** None.

## Poem 3: La Mar and La Alon-nes (The Sea and Solitude)

**VELA:**
Mi go tu mar at evning kol,
wave toka and toka wit no end.
Skai turna from orange tu gray,
and wind tucha mi skin slow.
No fren kom, no bird sing near,
onli watre and mi stap on sanda.
Mi tinka abau lifa, abau deta,
abau tru-nes, abau hop, abau mi self.
Mar stei big, mi stei smol,
bot in alon-nes mi herta lern peis.

**English:**
I go to the sea in a cold evening,
waves speak and speak without end.
The sky turns from orange to gray,
and wind touches my skin slowly.
No friend comes, no bird sings nearby,
only water and my steps on sand.
I think about life, about death,
about truth, about hope, about myself.
The sea stays vast, I stay small,
but in solitude my heart learns peace.

**New word:** `alon-nes` (solitude) — `alon` (alone) + `-nes`, ratified in R18. (Originally `solitud`, which ended in an illegal `-d` coda.)

## Poem 4: Tu Luv (Love)

**VELA:**
Yu si mi wen day starta low,
yu toka gud wen wailda fel hevi.
Mi hand holda yu hand in storm,
and afreda go daun from mi herta.
Wi woka long river at noita,
star and moon stei wit wi.
Yu lafa, mi lafa, wind dansi,
wi share bred, wi drinka watre.
If war kom, wi standa strong,
bikos tu luva giva wi fri lifa.

**English:**
You see me when the day begins low,
you speak goodness when the world feels heavy.
My hand holds your hand in the storm,
and fear goes down from my heart.
We walk along the river at night,
stars and moon stay with us.
You laugh, I laugh, the wind dances,
we share bread, we drink water.
If war comes, we stand strong,
because your love gives us a free life.

**New words:** None. Title "Tu Luv" = "your love" (possessive context).

## Grammatical Observations

1. **Tense consistency:** All poems use present (`-a`) for current actions, future (`-wil`) for anticipated actions.
2. **Word order:** Strict SVO maintained even in poetic register.
3. **Pitch accent:** Penultimate syllable naturally falls on content words (e.g., `rein-a` = [REI.na], `memori` = [me.MO.ri]).
4. **Emotional vocabulary:** `longin`, `alon-nes`, `fri` (free), `peis` (peace) work well in poetic context.
5. **Compounds:** `wud hors` (wood horse = wooden horse), `fri lifa` (free life) used descriptively, not as atomic compounds.
