## Story: A Bird Who Learns to Sing

### Paragraph 1 — The Broken Voice

**VELA:**
In wan smol fores neer river, wan smol birda liv-edo in wan olda wuda tre. Evri morne sun rais-edo, bird-en flai-edo ova gras and sing-edo brite sonj-en. Bot dis smol birda sit-edo kwaiata on brancha and her-edo dem. Wen li trai-edo sing, li vois kom-edo broka and shaka. Krauda bird-en turn-edo and si-edo li. Sam bird-en tok-edo, "Yu no sing, yu no flya sonja." Den dei laf-edo louda, and smol birda fel-edo sada and smol insaida li herta.

**English:**
In a small forest near a river, a little bird lived in an old wooden tree. Every morning the sun rose, birds flew over the grass and sang bright songs. But this little bird sat quietly on a branch and listened. When it tried to sing, its voice came out broken and shaking. The crowd of birds turned and saw it. Some birds said, “You do not sing, you cannot flai a song.” Then they laughed loudly, and the little bird felt sad and very small inside its heart.

---

### Paragraph 2 — The Night of Questions

**VELA:**
Noita kom-edo, moon shin-edo on watre, and smol birda no slip-edo guda. Li tink-edo, "Bikos wat mi no sing-edo? Bikos mi vois no strong?" Yestdei and tuodei sem pein kom-edo agen. Tuodei aft rein, li go-edo far from krauda. Li wok-edo daun hil tu wan kalma mautin wei, and li her-edo low vois from darka tre. Wan olda aul, biga ai and warma vois, stand-edo stil and si-edo li wit trasta.

**English:**
Night came, the moon shone on the water, and the little bird did not sleep well. It thought, “Why did I not sing? Why is my voice not strong?” Yesterday and today the same pain came again. That day after rain, it went far from the crowd. It walked down a hill to a calm mountain path, and heard a low voice from a dark tree. An old owl, with big eyes and a warm voice, stood still and looked at it with trust.

---

### Paragraph 3 — The Owl's Lesson

**VELA:**
Aul tok-edo, "Smol birda, yu wai krei-edo insaida?" Birda tok-edo, "Mi no sing-edo guda. Ol bird-en laf-edo. Mi trota no woka. Mi drim die-edo." Aul no laf-edo. Li kom-edo neer and tok-edo softa, "Sonja no starta in trota. Sonja starta in herta. Trota pas sonja, bot herta bringa tru vois. Yu no needa biga vois. Yu needa tru fieling and pasi." Smol birda her-edo and firsta taim in long day, li fel-edo wan smol hopa.

**English:**
The owl said, “Little bird, why do you cry inside?” The bird said, “I did not sing well. All the birds laughed. My throat does not work. My dream died.” The owl did not laugh. It came near and spoke softly, “Song does not start in the throat. Song starts in the heart. The throat passes the song, but the heart brings the true voice. You do not need a big voice. You need true feeling and patience.” The little bird listened and, for the first time in many days, felt a small hope.

---

### Paragraph 4 — The Practice

**VELA:**
From den, evri morne bifor krauda bird-en awaik-edo, smol birda weik-edo wit sun firsta lait. Li go-edo tu river side, stand-edo on ston, and brit-edo dipa. Li tink-edo abau winda, abau moter-se warma wing, abau moon on watre, abau pain and hopa tugeter. Den li sing-edo wan nota. Folo-wan morne, tu not-en. Folo-wan seven-dei, long sonja line. Sam day vois brok-edo agen, and li krei-edo. Bot li kontinyu-edo bikos aul tok-edo, "Pasi bringa flauor from hardi erda."

**English:**
From then on, every morning before the crowd of birds awoke, the little bird woke with the first sunlight. It went to the riverside, stood on a stone, and breathed deeply. It thought about the wind, about its mother’s warm wing, about moonlight on water, about pain and hope together. Then it sang one note. The next morning, two notes. The next week, a longer line of song. Some days the voice broke again, and it cried. But it continued because the owl had said, “Patience brings a flower from hard earth.”

---

### Paragraph 5 — The Singing

**VELA:**
Wan kol morne storma pas-edo and skai turn-edo klin blu. Smol birda stand-edo on hai brancha. Li no plan-edo show. Li onli fel-edo herta ful and open, so li sing-edo. Sonja kom-edo klia and warma, liki sun aft long noita. Not-en flai-edo ova fores, pas-edo river, and tuch-edo folia-n, ston-en, and watre. Bird-en stop-edo flai, stop-edo toka, and her-edo. Even winda wait-edo. Even olda tre seem-edo smile-edo in lait.

**English:**
One cold morning, after a storm passed and the sky turned clear blue, the little bird stood on a high branch. It had not planned to perform. It simply felt its heart full and open, so it sang. The song came out clear and warm, like sunlight after a long night. The notes flew over the forest, passed the river, and touched leaves, stones, and water. Birds stopped flying, stopped talking, and listened. Even the wind waited. Even the old tree seemed to smile in the light.

---

### Paragraph 6 — The Recognition

**VELA:**
Bird-en from evri side kom-edo neer. De si-edo dis smol birda and no biliv-edo firsta. Wan birda hu bifor laf-edo tok-edo low, "Dis sonja beautiful. Hu teach-edo yu?" Smol birda no proud-edo, no angri-edo. Li tok-edo, "Wan olda aul help-edo mi. Li lern-edo mi dat herta sing firsta." Den olda aul flai-edo daun from darka brancha and stand-edo baki-wei smol birda. Krauda bird-en bow-edo hed-en. Sam ai-n wet-edo wit hapi teer.

**English:**
Birds from every side came near. They saw this little bird and did not believe it at first. One bird who had laughed before spoke quietly, “This song is beautiful. Who taught you?” The little bird was not proud and not angry. It said, “An old owl helped me. It taught me that the heart sings first.” Then the old owl flew down from a dark branch and stood behind the little bird. The crowd of birds bowed their heads. Some eyes became wet with happy tears.

---

### Paragraph 7 — The New Forest

**VELA:**
From dat day, morne in dat fores chang-edo. Bird-en no laf-edo at broka vois agen. Wen young birda no sing-edo guda, de tok-edo, "Kontinyu. Yu herta grota." Smol birda and olda aul sit-edo tugeter at sun-raisa and her-edo new sonj-en from evri brancha. Fores fill-edo wit muzika and peis. And smol birda nou-edo dis tru-nes foreva: tru sonja no kom from trota alon; tru sonja kom from herta, and herta can make ol wailda hapi.

**English:**
From that day on, mornings in that forest changed. Birds did not laugh at broken voices again. When a young bird could not sing well, they said, “Keep going. Your heart is growing.” The little bird and the old owl sat together at sunrise and listened to new songs from every branch. The forest filled with music and peace. And the little bird knew this truth forever: true song does not come from the throat alone; true song comes from the heart, and the heart can make the whole world happy.

## Grammatical Analysis

### Tense System in Narrative
The story uses the VELA tense system consistently:

| Tense | Marker | Example | Context |
|-------|--------|---------|---------|
| Past narration | `-edo` | `liv-edo`, `flai-edo`, `tok-edo` | Most of the story |
| Present dialogue | `-a` | `no sing-a`, `start-a`, `bring-a` | Direct speech |
| Future | `-wil` | Not used in this story | — |

### Dialogue Marking
Direct speech uses `" "` quotation marks, standard in VELA orthography. The tense shifts from past narration to present dialogue — a natural register shift confirmed by grammar rules.
