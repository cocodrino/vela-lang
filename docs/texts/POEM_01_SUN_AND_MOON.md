## Original VELA Poem

**Title:** *La Sun and La Mòn*
*(The Sun and the Moon)*

```
La sun go up in la blu skai,
La mòn slip daun in la nait.
Wi si la starz, wi si la lait,
La erth tuen sutili, sutili.

La wind sing in la green tri,
La brid flai tu la morne.
Water run, snow mel, flaur open,
La werld liv in pasi.

Mi fiel la warm sun on mi skin,
Mi hier la sòft rán on la glass.
Yu es mi lait, yu es mi dai,
Wi go forti, tugeter.
```

---

## English Translation

The sun goes up in the blue sky,
The moon sleeps down in the night.
We see the stars, we see the light,
The earth turns softly, softly.

The wind sings in the green tree,
The bird flies to the morning.
Water runs, snow melts, flower opens,
The world lives in peace.

I feel the warm sun on my skin,
I hear the soft rain on the grass.
You are my light, you are my day,
We go forth, together.

## Grammatical Decisions Taken in This Poem

### 1. Adjective Placement
**Rule confirmed:** Adjectives precede nouns.
- `blu skai` (not *skai blu*)
- `green tri` (not *tri green*)
- `warm sun` (not *sun warm*)
- `soft rán` (not *rán soft*)

### 2. Pitch Accent in Poetry
**Decision:** Pitch accent (penultimate = HIGH) creates natural meter.
- `la sún` (sun — penultimate is the only syllable, so HIGH)
- `la mòn` (moon — same)
- `su.tí.li` (softly — penultimate `ti` = HIGH)
- `tu.gé.te.ru` (together — penultimate `te` = HIGH)

The pitch accent creates an iambic-like rhythm naturally, without forcing an artificial meter.

### 3. Verb Conjugation in Poetry
**Rule confirmed:** Present tense = root + `-a` (but in poetry, the `-a` can be dropped for meter if context is clear).
- "go" = `go` (imperative/bare root, context makes it present)
- "slip" = `slip` (bare root, poetic license — should be `slipa` in prose)
- "si" = `si` (bare root — should be `sia` in prose)
- "fiel" = `fiel` (bare root — should be `fiela` in prose)
- "hier" = `hier` (bare root — should be `hiera` in prose)

**Decision:** Poetry allows bare verb roots as a stylistic register. Prose requires `-a` suffix.
