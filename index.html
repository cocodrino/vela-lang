Chapter I

Four Principles, in Order

Simplicity, transparency, beauty, universality — voted into law, in that order.

Marginal note — a committee of five AI specialists (phonologist, morphologist, lexicographer, semanticist, aestheticist) votes on every rule before it enters the corpus. Twenty rules ratified so far, R1 through R20.

Most constructed languages ask you to memorise exceptions. VELA removes them. There is exactly one irregular verb — bi, “to be” — and everything else follows four principles, each weighed in order before the next is allowed to matter.

  1. I.

    Simplicity

    Rules you can count on two hands. One sound per letter, open syllables, a single irregular verb.

  2. II.

    Transparency

    If you know the parts, you know the whole. Compounds, suffixes and tenses are read, never guessed.

  3. III.

    Beauty

    Rules are necessary but not sufficient — the aestheticist's vote weighs how a word sounds and sits on the page.

  4. IV.

    Universality

    No single source language is favoured. Roots are built to be learnable from any first language.

Chapter II

What That Looks Like

Eight rules, each with its proof. No exceptions hiding underneath.

  1. 01

    Spelled as spoken.

    One letter, one sound. No silent letters, no th.

    Proof

    Helo!

    Hello!

  2. 02

    The dictionary form is the present tense.

    maka = to make / makes. Past adds -ed, future adds -wil, to the short root.

    Present

    Mi maka breda.

    I make bread.

    Past & future

    Mi mak-ed breda. / Mi mak-wil breda.

    I made bread. / I will make bread.

  3. 03

    One pronoun for he, she and it: li.

    No grammatical gender anywhere.

    Proof

    Li sing.

    He sings. / She sings. / It sings.

  4. 04

    Words you can read by parts.

    Compounds are transparent: lern-hous = learn-house = school.

    Proof

    lern-hous

    school

  5. 05

    One visual rule.

    A hyphen joins the short root; a space keeps the full word.

    Root, joined

    red-hous

    a red house

    Full word, spaced

    un reda bol

    a red ball

  6. 06

    Adjectives never change.

    un reda bol / La bol es reda.

    Proof

    un reda bol / La bol es reda.

    a red ball / The ball is red.

  7. 07

    Grammar by suffix, not by exception.

    Plural -n/-en, possessive -se, location -to, professions -po.

    Plural

    tu dog-en · tri tre-n

    two dogs · three trees

    Possessive & location

    Li-se hous es biga. / Wi livi la siti-to.

    Her house is big. / We live in the city.

  8. 08

    No passive voice.

    Say who did it.

    Proof

    Pipol maki-ed la hous.

    People built the house.

Chapter IV

One Root, Four Branches

A short root carries tense and trade alike, drawn here with compass and rule as a fan from a single hub.

mak — "make" — stays whole under every branch: a vowel for the present, -ed for the past, -wil for the future, -po for the person who does it.

Chapter VI

Sound and Measure

Five vowels, twenty consonants, and a stress that always falls in the same place.

Vowels

a/a/ e/e/ i/i/ o/o/ u/u/

Consonants

p t k b d g m n f v s z sh h l r w y ch j

Rules of the ear

  • Always the same. Every vowel keeps its sound; none is ever reduced to a schwa.
  • Never in VELA. No th, no zh, no "uh" schwa sound.
  • Open syllables. A word may end only in a vowel, or in n, m, l, r, s.
  • Pitch, not force. Stress falls on the second-to-last syllable, carried as melody rather than percussion.
